# The Tick on the Balcony

Late in the afternoon, Deniz was playing on the balcony with his little yellow truck. The floor was warm under his feet, and the sky was turning soft and orange. Then he heard it.

Tick.

One short little tick, high up somewhere. Deniz looked up. "Hmm," he said. "Who was that?"

He waited. Nothing. Then—

Tick.

And just as fast, it was gone, like a tiny sneeze in the sky.

Deniz set down his truck. He stood very still, listening with his whole body, his eyes big and round. “Where are you, tick?” he whispered.

He looked under his little chair. He looked behind the truck. He even peeked at the laundry line, swinging gently above. No, no, and no.

He tiptoed to the flowerpot near the door. He bent down, close to its rough edge, and listened. Nothing. He peeked at the small stones on the floor. He picked one up. “Was it you?” he asked. Then he tapped it gently against another.

Tick.

But that wasn't quite the same sound. And it wasn't the same place.

Then, from somewhere else — tick!

"Ah!" said Deniz. "You moved!"

He followed the sound step by step, all the way to the balcony railing. He stood close and listened carefully. A breeze came by, soft and cool on his cheeks — and tick! There it was again. The breeze faded. Deniz waited. Quiet.

Deniz looked up. There was a little ornament hanging from a hook — two small metal rings, shiny and bright, hanging side by side. The wind pushed one little ring, and it swung, and it swung, and it touched the other ring.

Tick!

Deniz watched and listened, watched and listened. Tick... tick... then nothing. The wind had stopped, and the rings hung still and quiet, like two sleeping fish.

He did not pull or touch the ornament.

Deniz held up his hand and gently waved his palm, back and forth, making a little breeze all his own. The small ring began to swing... swing... swing...

Tick! Tick!

Deniz laughed. "I made wind with my hand!"

Now it was a game. He waved his palm softly — tick, tick. He stopped — quiet. He waved again — tick, tick. The rings answered him every time. Sometimes they ticked fast, like tiny footsteps. Sometimes they ticked slow, like a sleepy clock.

Then Deniz tried his very own rhythm. He waved once — tick. He waved again — tick. Then he put his hands down, all the way down, and stood very still, waiting.

One long, quiet pause.

A slow breath.

Another slow breath.

The rings hung still. Deniz whispered, “Will you tick back? Will you tick twice, just like me?”

Deniz waited, and listened, and waited some more — listening for the wind's reply.
