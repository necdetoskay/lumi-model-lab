The Missing Blue Tag

Eli was five, curious, and patient. He stood beside the mint bed in the small community garden. A green watering can rested in his hands. On the other beds, colored plant tags stood in the soil. Eli saw a red tag and a yellow tag. But the blue tag for the mint was gone. Nora, a grown-up, sat nearby on the bench. She waited and let Eli look first. A short path ran beside the garden. Eli bent close to the mint. He saw an empty hole in the soil. Then he spotted a tiny blue fleck on the path. He did not water yet. He decided to look slowly.

Eli looked at the mint leaves. They were soft and bright. “Maybe the tag is hiding in here,” he said. He gently moved the leaves, one by one. He found mint stems and dark soil, but no tag. His guess was not right. Eli put the leaves back. Then he went to the short path and looked again. The tiny blue fleck was still there. Near it, he saw a faint line in the dust. It was thin, like a little pencil mark. Eli followed it with his eyes. The line went toward the bench. He walked slowly and stopped at the bench’s edge. Under it, he saw a tiny blue edge. Nora stayed on the bench. She did not point or reach. Eli looked from the empty hole, to the blue fleck, and then to the dusty line.

“I think I know,” Eli said. “The tag slipped out of the soil. Then it slid along the path.” He looked at the blue fleck again. He looked at the faint line. The clues matched. Eli bent beside the bench and reached under its edge. He did not crawl in. His fingers touched something smooth. He pulled it out. It was the missing blue tag. Dust covered one side, but it was safe. A small breeze moved the mint leaves. Eli watched them sway. Maybe the loose tag had been pushed along when the breeze came. It had traveled from the empty hole, across the path, and under the bench. Eli smiled. He had found it by looking closely.

Eli wiped the dust from the blue tag. He pressed it firmly into the soil beside the mint. Then he checked it. It stood straight. Nora smiled, but she let Eli do the checking. “Now the mint has its name,” Eli said. He lifted the green watering can and gave the mint a gentle drink. He watered the other plants, too. The red and yellow tags stayed bright in the sun. The garden was labeled and calm. Eli felt proud. Careful looking had helped him solve a small problem.
