The Tiny Tick on the Balcony

Late in the afternoon, Deniz was playing on the balcony. His little cars were lined up in a long, long row.

Tick.

Deniz looked up. What was that?

He waited. The balcony was quiet. Even the cars seemed to wait.

Tick.

There it was again! And then... nothing. Deniz stood up slowly. He turned his head one way, then the other. Where did the sound go?

He tiptoed to the big flowerpot. Maybe a little bug was ticking on the edge. He looked all around the rim. No bug. No tick. Just leaves.

He crouched down and peered at the small stones on the floor. Maybe a stone was ticking! He touched one. Cool and still. He touched another. Still and cool.

Tick!

That came from somewhere else! Deniz jumped up and followed the sound all the way to the balcony railing. He leaned in close.

The wind blew. Whoosh.

Tick.

The wind blew again. Whoosh.

Tick, tick.

Deniz held very, very still. No wind. No sound at all.

Then Deniz saw it. Up high, a small ornament hung from the balcony ceiling. It had strings and beads and two little metal rings. One ring was swinging in the wind. Swing... swing... swing. And when it swung close to the other ring —

Tick!

The two rings touched! That was the sound!

Deniz watched. The wind stopped. The ring stopped swinging. And the sound stopped too. Quiet. All quiet.

The wind came back. The ring swung again. Tick! The wind stopped again. Quiet again.

Deniz wanted to try. But he did not pull the ornament. He did not touch it at all. Instead, he held up his hand, flat like a little paddle. He waved his palm gently. Fff... fff... A soft little breeze rolled through the air.

The ring swung. Swing... swing...

Tick!

Deniz giggled. He did it again. Fff... fff...

Tick, tick!

Now it was a game! A slow wave made a slow sound. Tick... tick. A fast wave made a fast sound. Tick-tick-tick! Deniz waved and waved, and the little rings answered him every time.

Then Deniz had an idea. A rhythm idea.

He waved his palm twice, quick and small.

Tick. Tick.

Then he stopped. He put his hand behind his back and waited. One... two... three... The balcony was quiet. The ornament hung still.

Deniz waited some more.

And then — whoosh — the wind came blowing by. The little ring swung, swing... swing... and touched the other ring.

Tick. Tick.

The wind answered him!

Deniz laughed out loud. He waved his palm again.

Tick. Tick.

Then he waited, very still, listening with both ears.

And when the wind was ready, the little rings sang back.

Tick. Tick.
