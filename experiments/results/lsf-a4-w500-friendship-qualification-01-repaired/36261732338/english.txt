The Red Middle

The sun was warm. The courtyard stones were warm. Deniz and Ada sat down with their toy cars and their chalk.

Deniz drew a long blue road. It curved around a crack and wiggled to one side of the courtyard.

Ada drew a long yellow road. It went the other way, past the drain, around a leaf.

Two roads. Two cars. Vroom, went Deniz's car around the curve. Vroom, went Ada's car past the leaf.

Then Ada spied something by the chalk box. One little red chalk. The last one. Short as a thumb.

"Red," said Deniz.

"Red," said Ada.

Both hands shot out. Fingers bumped. Deniz held one end. Ada held the other end.

"I need red," said Deniz.

"I need it too," said Ada.

Each gave a tiny pull.

SNAP.

The little chalk broke in two. One red piece in Deniz's hand. One red piece in Ada's hand. Both hands went still. Nobody said anything.

Ada stared at her broken end. Deniz set his car down, slow, slow, and stared at his.

Then Deniz rubbed his red piece on the stones. Squeak, squeak. It still drew! A little red line, right there.

Ada tried her piece. Squeak, squeak. It drew too!

So Deniz made a small red mark on his blue road. Ada made a small red mark on her yellow road. Two little red marks. But still two roads, with a gap of plain stones between.

Ada rolled her car up to her edge. It stopped. Deniz rolled his car up to his edge. It stopped. The two cars faced each other, nose to nose, across the empty gap.

"No way across," said Ada.

Deniz looked at his red piece. Ada looked at hers.

Ada drew from her yellow road, out toward the middle. The red line grew, out and out, until it reached halfway across the gap. Ada lifted her chalk and looked at Deniz.

"Your side," she said.

So Deniz drew from his blue road, out toward the middle. His red line grew, out and out — and touched hers! Red met red, right in the middle.

One long red crossing, straight across the gap. From the blue road to the yellow road. Now there was one road, with a red middle.

"Test it!" said Ada.

Vroom. Her car rolled onto the red crossing.

Vroom, vroom. Deniz's car rolled on too.

Both cars went toward the middle. Closer and closer. They almost bumped! Nose to nose, right at the red middle.

Deniz stopped his car. "You go," he said, grinning.

Whooosh. Ada's car zipped past. Then she stopped it on the far side. "Your turn!"

Whooosh. Deniz's car rolled through.

On the stones beside the crossing lay the two broken red halves, side by side, short and stubby.

Then Deniz and Ada rolled their cars back for one more go. Both cars came toward the red middle. Both stopped. Then — whooosh — one car went through. And then — whooosh — the other.
