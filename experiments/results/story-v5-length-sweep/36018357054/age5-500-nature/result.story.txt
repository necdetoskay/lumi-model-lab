The Missing Blue Tag

Eli was five and very curious. He was also patient. He liked to look at small things. Today, he walked into the little community garden with an adult nearby. He carried no tools. A short path crossed the garden. A wooden bench sat beside it. Each garden bed had a colored tag. A red tag marked the tomatoes. A yellow tag marked the flowers. The mint bed had always had a blue tag. Eli picked up the watering can. It was ready for the plants. Then he looked at the mint. The blue tag was gone. Only green mint leaves waved in the warm air. “I do not see the blue tag,” Eli said. The adult stood near the path. “You can look while I wait here,” the adult said. “Take your time.” Eli set down the watering can. He decided to look carefully before he watered.

Eli knelt beside the mint. He looked at the empty place where the tag had been. There was a small hole in the soil. The tag stick had been pressed into that hole. Eli touched the dirt with one finger. It was soft. Then he walked to the short path. Near the path, he saw a faint line in the soft dirt. It was thin, like a little mark from something sliding. The line ran from the mint bed toward the bench. Eli followed it with his eyes. “Maybe the tag is in the watering can,” he said. He picked up the can and peered inside. Water shone at the bottom. A blue shape moved when Eli moved the can. He looked again. It was not the tag. It was the blue sky reflected in the water. Eli smiled at his mistake and put the can down. Then he went back to the dirt line. He followed it slowly to the bench. He bent low. Under the bench, a tiny blue corner showed in the shade. Eli knelt beside the bench. He remembered the hole by the mint and the line in the dirt. He began to think.

Eli looked from the blue corner to the faint line. The line began at the mint bed and ended near the bench. The empty hole showed where the tag had started. Eli thought for a moment. “The tag may have tipped over,” he said. “Then it slid across the soft dirt.” He reached under the bench. His fingers touched something smooth. He gently pulled. Out came the blue plant tag. A little dirt clung to its stick. Eli held it up. It was small and bright blue. The adult smiled from the path, but did not move to take it. “You found it,” the adult said. Eli felt proud. He had used the hole, the dirt line, and the blue corner to understand what happened. He carried the tag back to the mint bed.

Eli pressed the blue tag into the small hole beside the mint. It stood straight again. Red, yellow, and blue tags now marked all the beds. The adult helped Eli fill the watering can when it felt heavy. Then Eli watered the mint slowly. Drops darkened the soil. The blue tag stayed in place beside the green leaves. “Looking carefully helped you solve a small problem,” the adult said. Eli smiled. He was proud of his careful eyes. Then he carried on watering the garden.
