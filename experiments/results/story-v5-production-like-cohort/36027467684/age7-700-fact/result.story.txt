The Crooked Sprinkler

After the automatic watering cycle ended, Ela walked along the marked public path in HERB_WALK. She kept her feet behind the line and looked carefully at the garden beds. The mint bed glittered with water. Its leaves trembled under heavy drops, and the soil looked almost shiny. The nearby herb beds were damp, but only lightly damp. Their leaves held a few bright beads instead of a wet sheen.

Ela tilted her head. “Why does the mint get so much more water?” she asked herself. She did not want to fix anything or touch the garden. She only wanted to find the reason. Carefully, she decided to compare the beds side by side and then look at the visible sprinkler heads from the path.

Ela began with the mint bed and the next herb bed. Near the mint, she noticed a small puddly patch between the plants. Beside the neighboring bed, there was no matching patch. Ela crouched on the path, without reaching over the edge, and watched the last drops slide from the leaves.

The wettest part of the mint bed was not everywhere. It was on the side facing one sprinkler. The other bed was drier on the side facing its sprinkler. Ela looked from one bed to the other several times.

“Maybe that sprinkler is sending water farther in this direction,” she said. It was only a question, so she kept watching. The sprinkler heads were visible from HERB_WALK, and the path gave her a safe place to compare them. She would not touch, move, or test them herself.

Ela thought of another possibility. “Maybe the mint bed just has a low spot,” she guessed. A low place could hold water like a tiny bowl.

She checked from the path. The soil did not show one little pool at the lowest point. Instead, the extra wetness spread in a curved, spray-shaped area across the mint side. Then the sprinkler made one more visible burst. Water crossed toward the mint in a bright arc, while much less water reached the neighboring bed.

Ela watched the drops land. “So it is not only a low spot,” she decided. “The water is being sprayed there.” Her new observation made her first guess less likely.

Now Ela studied the visible sprinkler serving the mint side. She compared it with the neighboring sprinkler heads, looking from the marked path. The others stood upright and sent their sprays toward the centers of their beds.

But this sprinkler head leaned to one side. It was tilted crookedly. When it burst again, its water arced sideways over the mint instead of staying centered. Ela watched the arc, then looked at the wet, shining patch below it. She looked once more at the drier neighboring bed.

“The crooked sprinkler is watering the mint too much,” she said. “Its crooked angle sends extra water over here.”

The answer fit all the clues: the puddly patch, the spray-shaped wetness, the water crossing toward the mint, and the difference between the two beds. Ela felt pleased. She had not needed to touch anything or visit any place beyond the public path. Her careful comparisons had shown her what was happening.

A nearby adult was walking along the path, so Ela explained what she had seen. She pointed to the crooked angle, the spray path, the puddly patch, and the drier neighboring bed.

The adult listened closely. “You compared the beds very carefully,” the adult said. “I can pass your observation to the garden staff.”

The adult did not guess the answer for her. Ela had found it herself, and the evidence supported it. She stayed on the marked path and continued walking through Riverside Garden Habitat, feeling satisfied.

Small clues, compared carefully, had answered her question.
