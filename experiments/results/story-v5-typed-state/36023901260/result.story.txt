The Missing Blue Tag

Eli walked into the small community garden with an adult close by. He carried a watering can with both hands. Eli was five. He was curious and patient. He liked to look at every little thing. Near the garden beds stood red, yellow, and green plant tags. But the mint bed had no blue tag. Eli knew the mint by its small, bright leaves and fresh smell. The bench stood nearby. A short path ran beside it. Eli set down the watering can and paused. “I should find the blue tag first,” he said. Then he began a careful search.

First, Eli guessed the tag might be hiding under the mint leaves. He knelt down. He gently lifted one leaf, then another. He found soil and small stems, but no tag. Eli looked again. His first guess was not right. He stood and walked slowly along the short path. Near the bench, he saw a little blue mark on the path. It was just a small streak. Eli bent close to see it. Then he noticed a loose leaf moving in a light breeze. The leaf slid a tiny bit toward the bench. Eli watched. The breeze was soft, not strong. “Maybe it moved something this way,” he said. He followed the blue mark toward the bench, wondering if the missing tag had traveled there.

Eli looked beside the bench. Nothing was there. Then he looked under it. A small blue edge peeked out from the shade. Eli smiled. He remembered the blue mark on the path. The tag must have slid over the path. He remembered the loose leaf moving toward the bench. The light breeze may have pushed the tag along with it. Eli reached under the bench and gently pulled. Out came the original blue tag from the mint bed. The adult stayed nearby and watched quietly. Eli held up the tag. “I found it by looking carefully!” he said. Then he carried it back to the mint bed.

Eli placed the blue tag beside the mint bed. It stood with the red, yellow, and green tags. The adult stayed close while Eli lifted the watering can. He watered the mint carefully. Drops fell on the leaves and darkened the soil. Eli looked at the blue tag and smiled. “Looking closely helped me find it,” he said. The garden was ready. Eli felt proud and calm. Then he set the watering can down beside the neat row of plants.
