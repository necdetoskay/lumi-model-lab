The Little Cloud That Went Ting

The rain had stopped. The courtyard was wet and shiny, and the puddles held little pieces of the sky. The air smelled like wet stones.

Deniz came out and stopped. Right there, at knee height, floated a tiny cloud — gray and white, small as a pillow — bobbing gently beside an empty metal bucket.

"Well, hello," said Deniz. "Hello, little cloud."

He crouched down low and watched. Then he waved one hand near it, not touching, just saying hello. The little cloud drifted away — a whole handspan! — then drifted slowly back and settled above the bucket again.

Deniz took a quiet breath and made a low, soft hum.

"Mmmmm."

The little cloud shivered, all over, like a small wobble of jelly. And down came one raindrop, plip, into the metal bucket.

*Ting.*

Deniz stopped humming. The cloud grew still. No more drops came.

He waited a moment. Then he hummed again, low and gentle.

"Mmmmm."

Shiver. Drop. *Ting.*

Then he made no sound at all. He sat as still as a stone. No hum — no drop. The little cloud just floated there, quiet as quiet.

"One hum," Deniz whispered, "makes one drop."

He tried a little game. First he hummed once.

"Mmmmm." Shiver. *Ting.*

He paused. The cloud paused too.

Then he hummed twice, quick as quick: "Mm! Mm!" Shiver, shiver. *Ting, ting.*

One drop. A pause. Two drops. Just like the hums.

Deniz grinned and played some more. A long, slow hum made one long, slow shiver and one slow drop — *tiiing.* Three quick little hums made three quick little drops — *ting-ting-ting.* "Ting-ting-ting!" whispered Deniz. He changed only the timing: hum now, hum later, hum-hum together. He never touched the cloud. He never grabbed it or squeezed it or tried to keep it. He just hummed, and the little cloud shivered, and the metal bucket rang its little bell sounds.

Then, with no hum at all, the little cloud began to rise. Slowly, slowly — up past Deniz's knees, up past his tummy — toward a big soft evening cloud hanging pink and gray over the rooftops.

Deniz had not made a sound. The little cloud was rising all by itself.

He stood up. He did not reach for it. He just watched it float higher, small and white against the big evening sky.

"Are you going up there?" he called softly.

The little cloud bobbed, the way clouds do, and kept rising.

Before it floated too high, Deniz took a slow breath and hummed two slow notes.

"Mmmmm." Shiver. *Ting.*

"Mmmmm." Shiver. *Ting.*

Two final drops, one and then the other, ringing in the bucket.

The tiny cloud rose on — up past the rooftops, up and up — until it drifted into the big pink evening cloud and was gone.

Deniz stayed beside the metal bucket. He leaned down close and listened.

*Ting... ting...*

The two rings grew softer and softer, like tiny bells far, far away, until the courtyard was quiet and the evening sky glowed.
