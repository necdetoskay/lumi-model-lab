The Red Chalk

Deniz and Ada sat on the courtyard floor with their toy cars. Deniz drew a long gray road with a piece of blue chalk. His road curved around a bumpy crack and swooped off to the left. Ada drew her road with yellow. Her road curved around a daisy and swooped off to the right. Two roads. Two cars. Vroom, vroom.

Then Deniz saw it. The last piece of red chalk, lying short and stubby by the watering can.

"Red!" said Deniz.

Ada saw it too. "Red!" said Ada.

They both reached. Their hands met around the little red stick.

"I need red," said Deniz. "Red is for the crossing on my road."

"I need it too," said Ada. "Red is for the crossing on mine."

Each one held an end. Each one gave a little pull.

SNAP.

The chalk broke right in two. Both of them stopped. Ada looked down at one broken end in her hand. Deniz set his car down slowly and looked at the other broken end in his.

Then Deniz looked closer. Two pieces. Two real, usable pieces of red chalk.

"Here," he said, and slid one piece across the floor to Ada.

Ada picked it up and turned it in her fingers. "Thank you," she said.

Deniz drew a small red mark on his road — a little square, like a crossing sign. Ada drew a small red mark on her road, too.

Now the roads came closer. Closer and closer. But they still stopped. Between them lay a gap of plain gray pavement, wide as a hop.

Ada rolled her car up to her edge. Deniz rolled his car up to his edge. The two cars sat there, nose to nose, with the empty space between them.

"They can't get across," said Ada.

So Ada drew a red line from her road, out into the middle of the gap. Then Deniz picked up his red piece and kept the line going from his side. Red from Ada. Red from Deniz. Red meeting red in the middle. The two broken chalk halves made one long red crossing, and the two roads were one road now.

"Let's test it!" said Deniz.

Ada rolled her car onto the red crossing. Deniz rolled his at the very same time. The cars zoomed toward the middle — and almost bumped! Bump-to-be!

Deniz stopped his car just in time. "You go," he said.

Ada's car crossed the red middle and rolled out the other side. Then she stopped her car and waited. Deniz's car zoomed across too. Vroom!

Beside the crossing, the two broken red chalk halves lay side by side, like two little red pillows.

Then Ada rolled her car back toward the red middle. Deniz rolled his toward it too. Closer and closer... and then they stopped, right at the edges.

"Ready?" said Deniz.

"Ready," said Ada.

Ada's car went through the middle — vroom! — and then Deniz's car went through, one after the other, across the red crossing they had made together.
