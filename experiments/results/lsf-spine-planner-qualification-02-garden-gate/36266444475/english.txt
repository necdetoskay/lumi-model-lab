The Tap-Tap at the Gate

Deniz was four years old, and he was playing in the courtyard, drawing circles in the dust with his toe. The sun was warm. The stones were warm. Everything was quiet and cozy.

Then he heard it.

Tap. Tap-tap.

Deniz stopped. He turned his head. His ears went wide open, like little doors.

"Tap-tap?" he said. "Who's doing tap-tap?"

The sound was tiny and far away, coming from the corner where the garden gate stood. Nobody had ever told Deniz exactly what was on the other side of that gate. It was a mystery. A secret. A maybe-something.

Deniz stood very still, the way explorers do. He held up one hand and counted the taps on his fingers.

Tap. Tap-tap. Tap.

"One, two, three, four," he whispered, bending down one finger at a time.

Then the breeze went away, and the sound stopped too. The courtyard was all quiet again.

But when the breeze came back — whoosh — the taps came back too!

Tap. Tap-tap. Tap.

"It's the wind doing it," said Deniz. "The wind is playing a game."

He decided to find out. He put his hands behind his back, like a proper explorer, and walked across the courtyard. Step, step, step. His shadow walked along beside him.

He stopped a few steps away from the garden gate. He did not touch it. He did not push it. He just looked.

The gate was closed, quiet and brown in the sunshine. Deniz looked up. He looked down. He looked left and right. Nothing was moving at all.

Then the breeze died away, and the tapping stopped.

Hmm, said Deniz with his eyebrows.

Then the breeze came back, rustling his hair, and — tap-tap — the tapping started again.

Deniz squinted and followed the sound with his eyes. Tap. There! Tap-tap. There again!

A thin little branch, low down on a bush beside the gate, was swaying in the breeze. Every time the wind pushed it, the branch swung forward and tapped its tip against the edge of the gate. Tap! Then it swung back. Then the wind pushed it forward again. Tap-tap!

"You're the tap-tapper!" Deniz said, laughing.

He reached out and caught the branch gently in his hand. He held it still.

No taps. The gate and the branch sat quietly together, like friends resting.

Deniz let go.

The breeze pushed, the branch swung — tap-tap! — and the tapping came back.

Deniz giggled. He did it again. He held the branch — quiet. He let go — tap-tap! He held it — quiet. He let go — tap-tap!

He made up a song: "Hold it, quiet. Let go, tap-tap. Hold it, quiet. Let go, tap-tap!"

The breeze kept blowing, and the branch kept tapping, and Deniz kept giggling at his own clever hands.

After a while, he waved at the branch. "Bye-bye, tap-tapper. Keep playing."

He walked back into the middle of the courtyard, clapping his hands in the same rhythm as he went. Clap, clap-clap. Clap, clap-clap.

And behind him, in the breeze, the little branch went on tapping softly against the garden gate. Tap. Tap-tap. Tap.
