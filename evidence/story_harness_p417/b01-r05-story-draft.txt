Mira sat by the window in her little house, with her chin resting on her hands. The night sky was dark and soft, and one small place in it looked empty. A star had gone missing.

"I wish you could come home," Mira whispered to the glass.

Her tummy felt wobbly, like jelly. Outside was big, and outside was dark. But Mira wanted to help the star even more than she wanted her tummy to stop wobbling. She took one small, brave breath.

"I will go and look," she said.

Slowly, slowly, she opened the door. Then she stepped out of her house and into the night.

Before long, Mira came to a meadow. She had never been there before, and the tall grass whispered all around her. Mira wanted to hurry, hurry, hurry. But she stopped instead. She stood as quiet as a mouse and looked carefully between the green blades.

And there, deep in the grass, something twinkled.

Mira knelt down and gently parted the grass. A glowing stone lay there, round and smooth. Whose stone was it? Mira did not know. So she did not say it belonged to anyone, not even to herself.

"Hello, little stone," she said softly. "I will take good care of you."

But the stone was only glowing a tiny bit, like a sleepy eye. Mira waited and waited. The stone did not wake up by itself.

"Oh," said Mira. "You need some help."

She lifted the stone in both hands. It felt warm, like a cup of milk. Mira hummed a gentle lullaby.

"Hush, little light, the night is kind. Shine a little, I am right behind."

The stone glowed a little brighter. It was only a little, but it happened because Mira kept trying.

Mira did not want to wander around the meadow. She wanted to bring the light back to her house. So she held the stone close and took tiny, careful steps, so that she would not drop it. The glow spread on the ground like a ribbon of gold, and the ribbon pointed the way home. Mira followed it, one small step after another, and left the meadow behind.

When she got home, Mira did not hide the stone in a pocket or under her pillow. A light is nicer when it is shared. So she set the stone down by the window, where anyone in the dark sky could see it.

The stone shone softly. Mira sat down beside it and waited, with her hands in her lap.

Would the star come back tonight? Mira did not know yet. But the window was glowing, the way was open, and Mira stayed close, keeping the little light company.
