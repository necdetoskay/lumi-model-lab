They hurried back toward the bridge Arda had crossed.

The bridge could not hold much longer.

They came down through the mist-filled trees at a dead run and found the great root span still hooked to its anchor on the far side, its near anchor torn loose, thrashing in the wind above a drop into the cloud sea. Mist rolled around their knees, cold and fast.

"Line," Nisa said.

No argument this time. Just the word.

Arda uncoiled the route line he'd carried from the shore. He tied one end around the last living trunk on his side and pulled the knot tight. Then he clipped a loop of the line to his harness, so that it ran from the trunk to him and he could pay it out as he went. He threw the coil across the gap. The wind bent it sideways, but on the second throw Nisa caught it. She wrapped it around her forearm and hauled herself across hand over hand while the wind tried to peel her off. She made it. She turned, braced her boots against the ledge, and held the line steady for him.

Arda clipped on and started across. He got three-quarters of the way over before the far anchor of the bridge groaned.

He felt it, the shift, the give, the whole bridge lurching toward the drop with him still on it. The span sagged and swung. The line, still clipped to his harness, snagged on the splintering roots of the falling span and dragged him backward along it, toward the edge.

"Arda!" Nisa shouted. "Now! Cut it now!"

There was only one thing left to do, and he hated every second of it. The line was the only marked way home, and he had carried it all the way from the shore. He drew his knife, sawed through the line beside his harness clip, and let the cut end whip away with the bridge.

The tension released at once.

The bridge fell away beneath him into the cloud sea. Arda lunged for the ledge where Nisa knelt. She leaned out and hauled. His hand found her wrist, and her grip found him.

Cutting the line had freed him from the falling bridge. Her strong grip, and his trust that she would be there the moment he let go, had done the rest.
