The Missing Pattern

Deniz stepped into the learning room. Maps lay on a table beside labeled shells, a little schedule, a pencil, and tape. On the wall, cards made a row: blue circle, yellow square, blue circle, yellow square, then a blank space. Three cards waited below: blue circle, yellow square, and red triangle. Deniz looked at the empty place. “I want to find the right one before map time,” Deniz said. “I will look first.”

Deniz picked up the red triangle. It was the only shape not seen twice. “Maybe this one!” Deniz said. The triangle was a map sign too, so it seemed like a good choice. But Deniz pointed along the row. “Blue circle, yellow square. Blue circle, yellow square. Blank.” Deniz stopped. The pair came again and again. After yellow square came blue circle—not a red triangle. Deniz put the triangle down. “Not this one.” The blue circle and yellow square were still waiting.

Deniz looked at the two cards. Under the blue circle, a label said, “Water stop.” Under the yellow square, it said, “Tool stop.” The map’s little legend showed a blue circle by the water place. A yellow square sat by a box of tools. They were not the same place.

Deniz touched the cards. “I still have two,” Deniz told the room helper. “Which one goes here?”

The helper pointed to the schedule beside the display. “Look there,” the helper said. Deniz leaned close. The next words waited on the page.

Deniz looked at the schedule. It said, “Match the cards. Find water. Mark it with tape.”

Deniz looked beside the display. A pencil, a roll of tape, and the map waited there. “Match the cards. Find water. Mark it with tape,” Deniz said slowly.

Deniz looked back at the row. Blue circle, yellow square, blue circle, yellow square. The blue circle came next. It meant water. The yellow square meant tools.

“Blue circle finds water,” Deniz said. “Yellow square finds tools.” Deniz held the blue-circle card near the blank place, but did not put it down yet.

Deniz took a breath and placed the blue-circle card in the blank place. Now the row read: blue circle, yellow square, blue circle, yellow square, blue circle. “It fits,” Deniz said.

Deniz looked at the map. The blue-circle sign led to a water stop. The yellow-square sign led to the tool stop.

“Water,” Deniz said, remembering the schedule. “Find water and mark it.”

Deniz peeled a piece of tape and pressed it on the water stop.

The room helper came close. “Why not the triangle or yellow square?” the helper asked.

“The triangle breaks the row,” Deniz said. “The yellow square finds tools. The blue circle finds water.”

The room helper smiled. “Map time! Follow the blue-circle path to water.”

Deniz looked at the finished row: blue circle, yellow square, blue circle, yellow square, blue circle. Deniz had completed the display to prepare the map activity correctly. The children followed the blue-circle signs across the map and found the piece of tape at the water stop.

Deniz pointed to the cards. “We looked first. We did not just guess.”

The helper set the pencil beside the map. The children took turns following the labels, and the map activity began.
