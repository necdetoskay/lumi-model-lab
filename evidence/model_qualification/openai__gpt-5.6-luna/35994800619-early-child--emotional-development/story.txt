The Space Between Words

Deniz came to the community playroom, where Sam and helper Jo were familiar faces. Sam had invited Deniz to build their cardboard bus. They made vroom sounds together.

Then Sam said, “Wait, Deniz.” He carried the bus carefully behind a shelf and closed the little cabinet.

Deniz watched Sam whisper with Jo. Sam did not explain. Deniz’s tummy felt tight. Maybe Sam did not want to share the bus anymore.

Deniz touched their tummy. “It feels tight,” they whispered. “I feel left out.”

Behind the shelf, Sam held the cardboard bus. Its little door hung loose. A glue bottle sat on the floor.

Jo, the familiar helper, looked over. “What happened?” Deniz asked.

“Sam is fixing something,” Jo said. “You can ask Sam.”

Deniz watched Sam hold the door carefully. Maybe Sam was fixing the bus. Maybe Sam still wanted to keep Deniz away. The worry stayed, small and tight, inside Deniz.

Deniz peeked around the shelf. Beside the loose bus door was a dry spot, just the right size for Deniz’s red wheel.

Sam whispered to Jo, “Deniz can add the wheel when it is dry.”

Deniz looked at the closed little cabinet. Sam might want Deniz to help later. But the waiting still felt lonely.

Deniz took a small breath. “I felt left out,” they practiced. “Did you want me to wait?”

The words felt soft and ready. Deniz held the red wheel and waited for a chance to ask Sam.

Sam came around the shelf, carrying the cardboard bus. “I fixed the door,” Sam said. The door stayed on now.

Deniz looked at the little cabinet. It was still closed between them.

“I felt left out,” Deniz said. “I thought you did not want me.”

Sam held the bus carefully. “I wanted to fix it first.”

Deniz looked at the dry spot by the door. “I wanted to help.”

For a moment, neither spoke. The bus was fixed, but the hurt was not. Deniz still held the red wheel. They stayed beside the cabinet, ready to talk more.

Deniz took a small breath. “I felt left out when you said wait and closed the cabinet. I wanted to help.”

Sam looked at the cabinet. “I’m sorry. I wanted to fix the door for our bus. I thought wait meant you wanted me to do it.”

“I’m sorry I thought you did not want me,” Deniz said.

“I did want you,” Sam said. “I just wanted to fix it first.”

They opened the cabinet together. Sam held the bus steady. Deniz pressed the red wheel into the dry spot. Click! The bus stood ready. Deniz and Sam smiled and pushed it onto the playroom floor.

Deniz and Sam climbed into the cardboard bus and pushed it across the playroom rug. “Beep, beep!” Sam laughed. Then Sam stopped. “I need a minute.”

Deniz looked at Sam. “Do you want help or wait?”

“Wait, please,” said Sam. “Then you can drive.”

“Okay,” said Deniz. Deniz waited beside the bus. Soon Sam patted the seat. “Ready!”

Deniz climbed in, and together they drove across the familiar community playroom to Jo. “The bus is here!” they called. Jo waved, and the friends began their happy bus game again.
