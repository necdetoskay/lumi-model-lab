The Missing Pattern

Deniz came into the learning room. “I will get the table ready before the children come,” Deniz said. On the table lay a map, little labels, a schedule, and tools: a ruler, pencil, and tape. The map showed tree, bridge, tree, then a blank place. Labels sat below the pictures, but one label place was empty. A tree object and a bridge object waited nearby. Either one might fit.

Deniz looked at the empty label place. “Maybe someone took the label,” Deniz said. Then Deniz checked the tidy table. The tree and bridge objects were still there. Nothing else was out of place. “I will look,” Deniz said. The schedule lay beside the map. Deniz lifted one corner. Under it was the missing label! The schedule had been covering it. Deniz picked it up and placed it near the other labels. “I found it,” Deniz said calmly. But the blank map place still waited.

Deniz touched the map with one small finger. “Tree, bridge, tree, blank,” Deniz said. The green tree picture came first. The blue bridge picture came next. Then green tree came again. “Maybe tree goes here,” Deniz said, pointing to the blank. Deniz looked at the schedule, the one that had covered the label. Its marks went green, blue, green, blue. Green meant tree time. Blue meant bridge time. Deniz looked back at the map. Tree, bridge, tree—so bridge should come next. “Not tree,” Deniz said. “Bridge.” Deniz held the recovered label nearby, but did not place it yet. More checking was needed.

Deniz picked up the little ruler from the table. It was an ordinary tool for checking. Deniz laid it beside the map spaces. Each space was the same length. “Tree, bridge, tree, bridge,” Deniz said. The schedule showed green, blue, green, blue. The recovered label had a blue mark and said “bridge.” Deniz held it beside the blue bridge object. They matched. The map, the schedule, and the label all pointed to bridge. Deniz reached toward the blank place, then stopped. “Wait,” Deniz said. “I need to make the table ready.”

Deniz looked at the blank place. “The map says tree, bridge, tree, bridge,” Deniz said. “The schedule says green, blue, green, blue. The blue label matches the bridge.” Deniz picked up the bridge, not the tree. Carefully, Deniz set the bridge in the blank map place. Then Deniz put the blue “bridge” label in its place beside it. The row was whole: tree, bridge, tree, bridge. The spaces lined up, too. Deniz pressed the label flat and set the tree beside the map. “Now children can see what goes next,” Deniz said. Deniz checked the table once more. The map and its matching objects were ready.

Soon, the children came to the table. Deniz pointed to the row. “Tree, bridge, tree, bridge,” Deniz said. One child picked up a tree. Another picked up a bridge. They looked at the map and put each object in the matching place. No one felt stuck. The children smiled when the pattern worked. Deniz smiled too. “We looked,” Deniz said. “We checked. Then we chose. We did not just guess.” The map, objects, and labels were ready for everyone to use.
