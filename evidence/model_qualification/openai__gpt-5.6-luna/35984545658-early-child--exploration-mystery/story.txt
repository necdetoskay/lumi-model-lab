The Quiet Signal

In a coastal town, Deniz walked through the public garden beside the library with Grandma. Near the old observation point, Deniz heard, “Tap, tap, tap.” The sounds were soft. Deniz looked up. The wooden notice board moved once.

“Did you hear that?” Grandma asked.

Deniz listened. A moment later came three more taps. Deniz drew three small marks on paper. “I want to find out what makes that quiet signal,” Deniz said. Was it a message, or something else?

At the library, Deniz showed the librarian a paper with three marks. Grandma stayed close.

“What is on your paper?” asked the librarian.
“Three taps. The board moved.”

The librarian opened a simple town map. “Where did you hear them?” Deniz pointed to the garden. A blue path led to the old observation point.

“What is beside the path?”
“The sea!” said Deniz. “The board faces the coast.”

There, Deniz saw three old nail marks. “Maybe they matter.” Deniz carried the drawing and map to the nearby workshop.

The workshop maker welcomed Deniz. Deniz showed the drawing and map.

“Can loose wood make taps?” Deniz asked.

“Let us look,” said the maker. She held a thin paper strip near an open door. The strip fluttered. When she closed the door, it rested.

“Air can move paper,” said Deniz. “Maybe it can move wood, too.”

The maker pointed to the drawing. “What do you see?”

“A loose corner,” said Deniz.

“Good looking,” said the maker. “We will watch and listen safely. We will not climb or fix it yet.” Deniz nodded. The sea-facing board still needed a careful check.

At the old observation point, Deniz and the maker watched from the ground. A sea breeze lifted the loose corner.

Tap, tap, tap.

When the air grew still, the board grew quiet. The maker gently held the corner while the breeze blew. No taps came. Deniz saw white salt marks where the wood rubbed.

“The breeze may move the corner,” said Deniz. “Then the moving wood may make the taps. I should show the maker what I noticed before anyone repairs it.”

Deniz looked at the breeze, the loose corner, the quiet pause, and the salt marks. Together, they made a careful, strong clue. The board was not repaired yet.

Back at the workshop, Deniz spread out the drawing and map. “The board faces the sea,” Deniz said. “The breeze moved the loose corner. Tap, tap, tap. When the maker held it still, the taps stopped. These white salt marks show where the wood rubbed.”

The maker nodded. “You watched each clue.”

Only then did the maker safely secure the loose corner. Deniz stood nearby and listened. The breeze blew, but the board stayed still.

Deniz helped place a picture note on the board. It showed a breeze, a loose corner, and three little taps. Under it were the words: “Wind makes the taps.”

Deniz smiled. The quiet sound now had a clear answer.

Deniz walked through the garden. The sea breeze danced in the leaves. Swish, swish! The wooden board stayed quiet. Deniz smiled and read the picture note aloud: “Wind makes the taps.”

At the library, Deniz showed the drawing to the librarian. “I heard three taps,” Deniz said. “I saw the board move. The map showed the sea. The salt marks showed rubbing. When the maker held the corner, the taps stopped. Then the corner was fixed.”

The librarian smiled. Deniz felt calm and proud. Watching carefully had made the quiet sound clear.
