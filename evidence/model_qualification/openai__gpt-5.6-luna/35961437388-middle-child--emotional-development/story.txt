The Space Between Words

Every Wednesday, Deniz and Ece met at the neighborhood library after school. They knew which table had the best light and where the colored markers were kept. Their word-wall display was almost finished. Only a few cards needed placing.

Deniz held up a card with “curious” written in thick blue letters. “This one goes near the questions section.”

Before Deniz could reach the wall, Ece took it.

“Wait,” she said. “We’ll talk about this later.”

“Talk about what?” Deniz asked.

Ece tucked the card into her folder, then crossed to the supply cabinet. Ms. Selin, the librarian, was sorting paper there. Ece leaned close and whispered. Ms. Selin nodded. Neither looked toward Deniz.

Deniz stood beside the half-finished display, holding an empty strip of tape. The heater hummed, pages turned, and a cart squeaked past, but Deniz felt far away from everything.

Ece glanced back. “Just give me a minute, okay?”

Then she and Ms. Selin whispered again.

Deniz saw the edge of the blue card between the folder’s pages. Ece had removed it. Ece had hidden it. Those were things Deniz had actually seen.

But what did they mean?

Maybe Ece thought Deniz’s writing was bad. Maybe she wanted to finish the display without Deniz. The thought pressed against Deniz’s chest. It felt like being pushed out of a place that belonged to both of them.

Deniz wanted to ask, “Why are you acting like I’m not part of this?” Instead, Deniz squeezed the tape until it stuck to one finger.

The word wall still needed finishing. More than that, Deniz wanted to repair trust with Ece. They met here every week and had planned the display together.

So Deniz stayed near the table, watching the folder and waiting for an explanation.

Deniz stayed by the table while Ece and Ms. Selin finished whispering. When Ece carried a stack of books toward the reading corner, Deniz looked down at the supplies.

Beside the colored markers was a round water ring. A strip of fresh tape clung to the table. Under it lay a blank card and a used tissue.

Deniz leaned closer. The corner of the blue word card, still peeking from Ece’s folder, was smudged. The ink in “curious” had run into a fuzzy gray streak.

Deniz’s stomach tightened again.

The feeling was hurt, Deniz thought. Hurt was what was happening inside. “Ece is rejecting me” was a guess about why.

Those were not the same thing.

Deniz picked up the blank card, then put it down. Maybe water had spilled on the card. Maybe Ece had asked for a new one so the display would look neat. That would explain the tape and the supplies.

But it did not explain why Ece had hidden the card or said they would talk later.

Deniz carried the blank card to Ms. Selin, who was returning pencils to a cup.

“Did Ece ask for these?” Deniz asked, keeping their voice low.

Ms. Selin glanced at the card. “She asked for blank cards and tape. I gave her some.” Then she turned back to the pencils. “You two can sort it out.”

It was not an explanation. It was only another fact.

Deniz looked toward the reading corner. Ece was arranging books, her back turned. The easiest thing would be to tell someone else that Ece was being strange. But gossip would add guesses, not answers.

Deniz took a slow breath and pressed the blank card flat against the table.

“I’ll ask her,” Deniz whispered. Not, “Why did you do that?” Not, “Are you trying to leave me out?”

First, Deniz would ask what had happened. Then they could decide what the card needed—and what their friendship needed too.

Deniz waited until Ece finished stacking the books. Then Deniz took the blue card from the edge of Ece’s folder—not pulling, just lifting it when Ece set the folder down for a moment.

The card was wet-damaged, but the words were still there. “Curious” was blurred at one corner. The rest of Deniz’s writing remained clear. Ece had not thrown it away. She had kept the card flat inside the folder, safe from more spills.

That mattered.

It also did not erase the tight feeling in Deniz’s chest. Ece had moved the card without asking. She had hidden it. She had said they would talk later, while Deniz stood there wondering what was happening.

Maybe Ece had been trying to save the card. Maybe she had decided Deniz’s work was not good enough. Both ideas fit some of the clues. Only one might be true. Or there might be another explanation Deniz had not thought of.

Deniz put the card back exactly where it had been.

“I felt left out when I saw this,” Deniz whispered, testing the words. They sounded better than “You were leaving me out.” The first words told what was happening inside. The second ones guessed what Ece meant.

Deniz tried again. “I felt left out when I saw you take my card. Can you tell me what happened?”

The sentence felt wobbly, but fair. It did not pretend Deniz was fine. It also did not call Ece sneaky or mean.

Deniz carried the blank card and the blue card toward the word wall. The unfinished display waited above the table, with one empty space between two bright cards.

Ece looked up from the books. “Are you done with the—”

“Can we talk?” Deniz asked.

Ece’s hands went still.

Deniz held up the blue card, keeping a little space between them. “I saw this in your folder. I felt left out when I saw you take it. I want to know what happened before I decide what it means.”

For a moment, the library seemed unusually quiet. Deniz still felt hurt. The feeling had not vanished just because the card was saved.

But now the question was between them, where an answer could reach it.

Ece looked at the blue card, then at Deniz. Her fingers tightened around the edge of the table.

“Okay,” she said. “I can tell you.”

Deniz waited.

“Yesterday, I put my water bottle beside the supplies,” Ece began. “It tipped over. The water ran onto your card.”

Deniz glanced at the smudged corner. “You spilled it?”

Ece nodded. “I grabbed a tissue and dried it. I asked Ms. Selin for tape and a blank card because I thought I could fix the corner.”

“Then why did you take it away and hide it?” Deniz asked. The question sounded sharper than the one they had practiced, so Deniz took a breath. “I saw you whispering, and then I saw the card in your folder. I felt left out. I guessed you didn’t want my card on the wall anymore.”

“I understand why you guessed that,” Ece said. “But I didn’t want to throw it away. Your words were still good.”

She opened the folder and touched the blue card carefully. “I saw your note, too. The one that said, ‘Please ask before changing my cards.’”

Deniz remembered writing it after someone had moved a title card during their last project. It had been a boundary, not a threat.

“I was worried you’d be angry because I changed this without asking,” Ece continued. “So I thought I would repair it first and explain later. Then you came in, and I got more nervous. Hiding it made everything worse.”

Deniz looked at the card. The feeling in their chest was still there, but it had changed shape. “My hurt was real,” Deniz said. “But my guess that you were rejecting me wasn’t something I knew.”

“Right,” Ece said. “And the spill was an accident. But I still should have told you before moving your card or trying to fix it.”

Deniz nodded. “I should have asked instead of deciding what you meant.”

For a moment, they stood beside the empty space in the word wall.

“Can we fix it together?” Ece asked.

“Together,” Deniz agreed. “But let’s decide what stays the same first.”

They set the blue card, the blank card, and the tape on the table. The display was still unfinished, yet the next step no longer belonged to only one of them.

Deniz tapped the blue card. “The words are still mine. I want to keep the original sentence.”

Ece nodded. “May I copy the clear part onto the blank card and leave the smudged corner for us to rewrite?”

This time, Deniz noticed the question before answering. “Yes. And can we choose the new words together?”

“Together.”

Ece slid the blank card across the table. Deniz read the original sentence aloud while Ece copied it in blue marker. Most of the words were easy to see. At the corner, though, the ink had spread into a soft gray cloud.

“What did this part say?” Ece asked.

“‘A small question can open a big door,’” Deniz said. “The last few letters are missing.”

Ece held the marker above the card. “Should I write the whole sentence, or only the damaged part?”

“Only the damaged part. Then it will still look like my card.”

Ece wrote slowly. Deniz checked each letter against the clear words. When they finished, the sentence looked whole again. It was not exactly the old card, and it was not exactly Ece’s version. It belonged to both of them now because they had made the choice together.

They placed the repaired card in the empty space. Then they set the original, smudged card beside the display, where visitors could see the water mark and the careful repair.

Ece rubbed a blue stain from her thumb. “I’m sorry I hid the card. I should have told you about the spill instead of trying to fix everything secretly.”

Deniz looked at the empty tape roll. “I’m sorry I guessed you were rejecting me before I asked what happened. I saw things that hurt, but I didn’t know the whole story.”

Ece gave a small nod. “If one of us wants to change a card, we can ask first.”

Deniz pointed to the new sentence. “Maybe we can say, ‘Can we check this together?’ That way, the other person knows what is happening.”

“Can we check this together?” Ece repeated, testing the words.

Deniz smiled. “Exactly.”

They stepped back from the word wall. The cards lined up in bright rows, and the repaired one held its place without a strip of tape showing. Deniz still remembered the tight feeling from earlier, but now the finished card gave them something solid to look at: Ece had asked, Deniz had answered, and both had kept their promises. The display was ready for the library’s visitors.

The next Tuesday, Deniz and Ece met at the library after school. Sunlight lay across the word wall, making the blue letters shine. A few visitors had already stopped to read the cards, including the repaired one with its careful corner.

Ece stood on a small step stool, holding a yellow card near an empty space beneath the word “curious.”

Deniz felt a quick pinch in their chest. For one second, the old thought jumped up: Ece is changing something without me.

Then Deniz looked again. Ece was holding the card in both hands, not pressing it onto the wall. She had not moved any of the displayed cards.

“Can we check this together?” Deniz asked.

Ece turned and smiled. “I was hoping you’d say that. I think this card might fit better under ‘question’ than ‘curious,’ but I wanted to ask first.”

Deniz stepped closer. The card said, “A question helps us notice more.” Deniz read it twice. “I like the sentence. Maybe ‘A question helps us notice new things’ is clearer. The other cards use words people might say every day.”

Ece tried the new version aloud. “A question helps us notice new things.” She tilted her head. “Yes. ‘Notice more’ sounds like the question is wearing glasses.”

Deniz laughed. “Tiny word-glasses.”

They laughed softly, careful not to disturb the readers nearby. Then Ece handed Deniz a pencil, and Deniz wrote the new sentence on a clean label while Ece held the card steady. Together they placed it beneath “question,” then stepped back to look.

The card fit there better. Deniz still remembered how heavy the first misunderstanding had felt. Remembering it did not make every new moment dangerous, though. This time, Deniz had noticed the feeling, looked for what was actually happening, and asked.

Ece pointed to the repaired blue card. “It’s still my favorite.”

“Even with the tiny word-glasses?”

“Especially because of them.”

A child passing the display stopped to read the cards. Deniz and Ece moved aside together, ready to explain any word someone asked about.
