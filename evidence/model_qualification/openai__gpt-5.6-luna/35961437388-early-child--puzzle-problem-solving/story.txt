The Missing Pattern

Deniz was four years old. She held Grandpa’s hand as they walked into the learning room. Maps covered one wall. Baskets held shells, stones, and little wooden animals. Each basket had a picture label. A clock and a picture schedule hung beside a long table. A cup of short rulers and pencils sat nearby.

On the table was the Map Trail display. Deniz leaned close. Green paper hills and a blue paper river made a path across the board. The path showed a hill, then a river, then a hill.

Then came an empty white space.

Deniz touched the air above it. “What goes here?” she asked.

Beside the display lay a small wooden bridge. It was loose, not on the board. Deniz picked it up with both hands and set it down gently. It had tiny brown rails and two flat ends.

“Maybe this helps the trail,” Deniz said.

She looked at the map again. Hill. River. Hill. Blank.

The pictures seemed to be waiting for something, but Deniz did not know what yet. She looked at the picture labels on the baskets, the schedule, and the empty place. Nothing told her the answer right away.

Deniz pressed her lips together and thought. The next children would use the Map Trail soon. She wanted to make it ready.

“I will look,” she said. “Then I can finish it.”

She left the bridge beside the board and bent down to inspect the display. The blank space waited between the green hill picture and the loose wooden bridge.

Deniz looked at the empty white space again.

“Hill, river, hill,” she said, touching each picture. “What comes next?”

Beside the board was a shallow card tray. Its little picture label showed a square card. The picture schedule stood behind it, with a sun, a snack, and children building something.

Deniz bent down to look at the tray. There were cards with green, blue, and brown pictures. But no card sat in the empty place in the tray.

She looked behind the schedule.

A blue corner peeked out.

“Oh!” Deniz said.

She carefully moved the schedule a tiny bit and pulled out a card. It had a blue river picture, just like the blue river on the Map Trail. The card had not been gone. It had been hiding behind the schedule.

Deniz held it in both hands. She looked at the pictures on the board again.

Green hill. Blue river. Green hill. Then the empty place.

She whispered the pictures slowly. “Hill, river. Hill, river.”

Her eyes grew wide. The same two pictures were taking turns. After the second hill, the next picture should be a river.

Deniz held the blue card beside the white space. It looked as if it wanted to fit there.

“The river goes here,” she said.

She did not push it down yet. First, she looked at the loose wooden bridge beside the board. The bridge was brown, with tiny rails and two flat ends. It was not a picture card. It could not simply become the blue river picture.

Deniz set the river card gently beside the blank space and left the bridge where it was.

“Now I know this part,” she said. “But what is the bridge for?”

The Map Trail had one more question waiting.

Deniz looked at the brown bridge again. It rested beside the blue river part of the Map Trail.

“Maybe it goes with the river cards,” she said.

That seemed possible. The bridge was near the river, and the river card was blue. Deniz picked up the bridge with both hands and held it over the shallow card tray.

But the bridge was not a card. It had tiny rails and two flat ends. The cards were thin and flat.

Deniz looked at the little label on the bridge’s basket. It showed a bridge picture. Under the picture was the word bridge.

“Bridge,” Deniz said softly. “It says bridge.”

She looked at the river cards. Their label showed a blue river picture. The brown bridge did not match that label.

Deniz shook her head. “Not in here. That guess does not match.”

She carried the bridge back beside the Map Trail. A line of small dots began on the first green hill. The dots went across the blue river and ended at the second green hill.

Deniz bent close. She touched the dots one by one.

“Here is a hill,” she said. “Here is the river. Here is the other hill.”

Then she held the bridge above the dotted line. One flat end pointed toward the first hill. The other flat end pointed toward the second hill.

“Oh,” Deniz whispered. “The bridge goes over.”

She lowered it near the river, but stopped before setting it down. It might reach from hill to hill. She needed to check.

Deniz put the bridge beside the blue river again and looked at the tool cup. Short rulers and pencils stood inside. The ruler could help her see if the bridge was long enough.

The river card still waited beside the blank space. The dots still waited across the blue river.

Deniz smiled a little. “The card shows river. The bridge may make a crossing.”

She picked up a short ruler. First she would check the bridge. Then she would look again at the picture schedule.

Deniz held the short ruler in one hand and the wooden bridge in the other.

She set the ruler beside the bridge. Then she laid the ruler along the dotted path, from the first green hill to the blue river and on to the second green hill.

“Let me see,” she said.

The ruler reached from hill to hill. The bridge was about the same length. Deniz placed one flat end near the first hill and held the other end near the second hill. It reached across the blue river without a gap.

“It can go all the way,” she said.

She did not put it down yet. The river card still waited by the blank place. The green hills, blue river, and little dots were still on the map.

Deniz looked at the bridge’s basket label again. The picture showed a bridge. The word said bridge.

“Bridge,” she whispered. “Not a river card.”

She pointed to the route. “Hill, river, hill.” Then she pointed to the blue card. “River goes in the blank.”

The hill and river pictures made a repeating row. The map showed the river between the hills. The label showed what the wooden piece was. The ruler showed that it could reach across.

Deniz turned to the picture schedule behind the card tray. The next picture showed a blue river with a little bridge over it. Under the picture were the words Build a Crossing.

Deniz looked from the schedule to the bridge. Then she looked back at the dotted line.

“Build a Crossing,” she said slowly. “The bridge goes over the river.”

She almost carried the bridge to the object tray. That was where loose things belonged. But the picture schedule showed a crossing, and the map showed a place for one. The ruler had shown that the bridge fit from hill to hill.

Deniz held the bridge above the blue river once more. She had checked the pattern, the map, the labels, and the schedule. Now she knew what the bridge could do.

She kept it in her hands, ready to place it. The river card was still beside the blank. The Map Trail was almost ready.

Deniz looked at the river card beside the blank place.

“Hill, river, hill,” she said. “Then river.”

She touched each picture with one small finger. The green hill came first. The blue river came next. Then the green hill came again. The same little row was ready to repeat.

Deniz slid the blue river card into the blank space. It fit neatly beside the last green hill.

“Now the pattern is done,” she said.

But the wooden bridge was still in her other hand.

Deniz looked at its label. The label had a bridge picture and the word bridge. She looked at the map. Dotted lines ran from one green hill, across the blue river, to the other green hill.

She laid the short ruler along the dotted way. The ruler reached from hill to hill. The bridge was just as long. It could stretch across the blue river.

Deniz held the bridge over the river. One flat end touched the first hill. The other flat end reached the second hill.

“It goes across the river,” she said.

Then she looked at the picture schedule. It showed a blue river with a little bridge on top. Under it were the words Build a Crossing.

Deniz thought about the object tray. Loose things went there. She could carry the bridge back and leave the map without it.

But the schedule needed a crossing. The dotted map showed where the crossing went. The bridge label told what the wooden piece was. The ruler showed that it reached. The repeating pattern had told her where the river card belonged.

Deniz shook her head.

“No tray,” she said. “The bridge goes across the river.”

She lowered it gently onto the map. The bridge rested between the two green hills, over the blue river and across the dotted route.

Deniz pressed one end, then the other. It stayed in place.

She stepped back. The Map Trail now showed hill, river, hill, river. A bridge crossed the blue river, ready for Build a Crossing.

Deniz smiled and gave the bridge a tiny tap.

“I checked,” she said. “Now it is ready.”

A room guide came to the Map Trail table and looked at the finished display.

“Oh!” she said. “The trail is ready.”

Deniz stood beside the table. The green hills sat on both sides of the blue river. The wooden bridge rested across it. The next children could walk their fingers along the dotted path and cross the river on the bridge. Everything was in its place.

The guide pointed to the cards. “How did you know where these went?”

Deniz touched the pictures one at a time.

“Look,” she said. “Hill, river, hill, river.”

Then she touched the bridge. “And the map had a line over the river. The bridge reached from hill to hill.”

She pointed to the picture schedule too. “It said Build a Crossing.”

The guide smiled. “You checked all the clues.”

Deniz nodded. She remembered when she had almost put the bridge in the river card tray. That guess had not matched the bridge label or the dotted path. Checking had helped her change her mind. She was glad she had stopped to look again.

The guide placed a small finger token at the start of the trail. “The next learners can begin here.”

Deniz watched as the token moved from the first hill toward the river. It had a bridge to cross because she had left the wooden bridge on the map. The bridge stayed steady between the two green hills.

Deniz felt warm and proud. She gave the bridge one gentle tap.

“First I checked,” she said. “Then I chose.”

The Map Trail waited, complete and ready for Build a Crossing. Deniz smiled as the next learners began to follow the path.
