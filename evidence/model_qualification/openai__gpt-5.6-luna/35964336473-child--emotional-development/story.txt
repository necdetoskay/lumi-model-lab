The Space Between Words

On Wednesday, Deniz hurried into the community reading room. It was a familiar place, with bright windows, low shelves, and the soft beep of the check-out machine. Lio was already waiting at their table.

“Ready to finish our welcome sign?” Deniz asked.

They had planned it together. The words would say WELCOME TO OUR READING ROOM, with neat spaces between each word. Deniz opened the box of colorful letter tiles.

Before Deniz could choose a W, Lio quickly lifted the box.

“Not yet,” Lio said.

Lio carried it behind a tall cabinet. Deniz followed with their eyes, not their feet. Lio bent down. Then Lio whispered, “Do not let Deniz see it.”

The words made Deniz’s stomach feel tight. Lio usually looked right at Deniz when they talked. Now Lio kept glancing away and moved the tiles faster and faster.

“Are you still making the sign with me?” Deniz asked.

Lio’s hands stopped. “Soon,” Lio said, but did not come back to the table.

Deniz looked at the empty space beside the paper sign. It felt as if the space had grown wide enough for another whole table. Maybe Lio did not want to work together anymore.

Deniz sat quietly, feeling left out, while Lio kept the tile box hidden.

Deniz stayed at the table, with both hands in their lap. The tight feeling in their stomach was still there.

“Left out,” Deniz whispered. That was the feeling. But a feeling was not the same as a fact.

Deniz thought about what had really happened. Lio had moved the tile box. Lio had whispered secret words. Lio had looked rushed and had not come back to the table. Those were facts. “Lio does not like me anymore” was a guess.

Deniz looked toward the reading-room notice board, not toward the cabinet. A new project sketch was pinned there. It showed the welcome sign they had planned together. Under the drawing were little colored squares for letter tiles. The words WELCOME TO OUR READING ROOM were written across the top, with spaces marked between them.

Deniz blinked. The sketch was Lio’s handwriting. Lio had planned to use the same colorful tiles. So the hidden work was still connected to their sign. That did not explain why Lio had taken the box or why the words had been secret. It did show that Lio had not thrown away their plan.

Deniz’s hurt did not vanish. Maybe Lio wanted the sign to be different. Maybe Lio had another reason. Deniz almost walked over to ask, but stopped. Asking while feeling hot and wobbly might make the words come out sharp.

“I need one more clue,” Deniz decided. Then Deniz watched quietly from the table, without peeking behind the cabinet or telling anyone else what had happened.

Deniz waited at the table and watched without peeking. After a while, Lio came out from behind the cabinet. The tile box was tucked under one arm. In the other hand, Lio held a piece of cardboard covered with a cloth.

Lio set the cardboard on the table and lifted one corner of the cloth. Deniz saw bright letters already lined up:

WELCOME TO OUR

The letters matched the welcome sign in the project sketch. But empty spaces showed where some words should go. The last word was missing too.

Deniz’s chest felt warm and pinchy. The sign was still their sign, or part of it. That was one clue. But Lio had taken the tile box, whispered secret words, and kept hiding the work. That was another clue. A secret could be exciting. It could also mean, “You are not included.” Deniz still did not know which one this was.

Lio glanced up and saw Deniz looking. Quickly, Lio pulled the cloth over the cardboard.

“Almost ready,” Lio said.

The words were not mean, but they did not answer anything. Deniz’s hands tightened in their lap. The worried feeling stayed.

Deniz thought about saying, “You do not like me anymore.” But that was a guess, not something Deniz had seen. Deniz needed to ask about what had really happened.

When the sign was ready, Deniz would say, “I felt left out when you hid my tiles. What happened?” Then Deniz would listen. The question felt scary, but it was clearer than a guess. Deniz watched Lio cover the sign and waited for the next clue.

Deniz kept the question ready in their mind. “I felt left out when you hid my tiles. What happened?” Deniz whispered it once, then tried again more quietly. It sounded better than, “You do not like me anymore.” That was a guess. Deniz wanted to listen before answering.

Lio carried the covered cardboard from the cabinet to the table. The tile box was still tucked under one arm. Lio set the sign down but kept the cloth over it.

“Can I tell you something?” Lio asked.

Deniz nodded, though their stomach felt wiggly.

“I borrowed your tiles without asking again,” Lio said. “I should have asked. And when I said, ‘Not yet,’ I did not explain well.”

Deniz looked at the cloth. “Why did you hide them?”

Lio held the edge of the cloth, but did not lift it. “I have a surprise plan. It is almost ready. I can’t tell you yet.”

The word surprise made Deniz wonder. It also left the same empty space in the middle of the worry. A surprise could be kind. Hiding the tiles had still made Deniz feel left out.

“I can wait until the sign is ready,” Deniz said. “But I want to ask you then.”

“Okay,” Lio said. “When it is ready, I will tell you.”

Deniz did not feel all better. Lio had borrowed the tiles and used unclear words. Those things were real. Still, Lio had brought the covered sign back instead of running away. Deniz sat beside the table and watched Lio smooth the cloth. Soon, they would uncover it and talk.

Lio took a slow breath and lifted the cloth.

The sign underneath was bright and neat. It read:

WELCOME TO OUR READING ROOM

At the bottom, a small row of shiny tiles spelled DENIZ.

Deniz stared. “That is for me?”

Lio nodded. “I wanted it to be a surprise. I used tiles from your box for the words, but I left the spaces and your name until the end. I needed the box so I could finish everything when you were not looking.”

Deniz looked at the sign, then at the tile box. The pieces behind the cabinet suddenly made more sense. “That is why you said, ‘Not yet’?”

“Yes,” said Lio. “You were coming back from the bookshelf, and I was still working. I hurried behind the cabinet. I said, ‘Do not let Deniz see it.’ I meant the sign, not that I wanted you to go away.”

Deniz touched the edge of the cardboard. “I felt left out when you hid my tiles. I thought maybe you did not want to work with me anymore.”

Lio looked down. “I understand. I made it look that way. I should have asked before taking the box, and I should have used clearer words.”

Deniz listened. The hurt was still there, but now it had a place beside the new facts.

“I should have asked what was happening instead of deciding what you meant,” Deniz said. “But I was really worried.”

“I’m sorry,” said Lio. “Next time, I can say, ‘I am making a surprise. May I borrow these?’”

“And I can say if I feel left out,” Deniz replied.

Together they placed the last blank spaces between the words. Deniz checked each one. Lio pressed the shiny name tiles firmly at the bottom.

“WELCOME TO OUR READING ROOM,” they read aloud.

The sign was ready. Lio picked it up, and Deniz carried the tile box. Side by side, they walked toward the reading-room wall to hang their work.

The reading-room wall had a clear spot beside the big window. Deniz held one side of the sign while Lio held the other.

“Up a little,” said Deniz. “There.”

They pressed the sign onto the wall. Then they stepped back.

WELCOME TO OUR READING ROOM

The spaces between the words were wide and easy to see. Every letter stayed in place. At the bottom, the shiny tiles spelled DENIZ.

A woman choosing a book smiled at them. “What a cheerful sign!”

Deniz smiled too. The sign made the room feel ready to welcome everyone.

When they returned to the table, Lio opened a small paper bag. Deniz saw a quiet worried feeling flicker inside. Lio was reaching toward the colored stars beside the tile box.

This time, Lio stopped.

“I am making a little star border for the sign,” Lio said. “May I use three of your blue stars?”

Deniz looked at the sign, then at the stars. Lio had explained what was happening. Lio had asked first.

“Yes,” Deniz said. “You can use three. I’ll count them.”

Lio counted carefully and placed the stars in a neat row. Deniz added two yellow ones beside them.

“Now it needs these,” Deniz said.

Lio grinned. “Good idea.”

Together, they looked up at the finished sign, bright against the wall. People who entered the reading room could see the words, the spaces, and the little shining name at the bottom.

Deniz felt sure about the next project. This time, Lio told the plan first. Then Deniz pulled the tile box closer, ready to help.
