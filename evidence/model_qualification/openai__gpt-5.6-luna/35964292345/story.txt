The Quiet Signal

Four-year-old Deniz sat by the quiet library window. Beyond it were the blue coast and the old observation point on a hill.

Tap, tap, tap. A pause. Tap, tap, tap.

Deniz looked out but could not see what made the sound.

“Please help me listen,” he told the librarian.

He tapped the rhythm on his knee.

“Let’s remember it first,” she said. “We won’t guess.”

Deniz watched the hill. Did the taps mean help?

Deniz went to the public garden with the librarian nearby. A strong sea breeze came. Leaves shivered, and a garden flag waved toward the old observation point.

Then: tap, tap, tap. A pause. Tap, tap, tap.

Deniz pointed. “Wind first. Taps next.”

They watched again. The breeze came. Leaves moved. The flag waved. Then came the same taps.

Deniz turned toward the workshop side of the old observation point. The sound seemed to come from there, but no source showed itself.

“Is it asking for help?” Deniz asked. “We need to look more.”

Deniz and the librarian walked to the nearby workshop. Deniz told the workshop guide, “Wind first. Leaves move. Then tap, tap, tap.”

The guide showed Deniz a drawing of the old observation point. At its top was a weather vane. Beside it, a dark rubbing mark crossed the wooden post.

“Maybe a metal edge touches the wood three times,” said the guide. “This is a good idea to test, not proof yet.”

Deniz looked toward the observation point. “Is it asking for help, or is it only moving?”

“We must watch there,” said the guide.

At the old observation point, Deniz stood beside the workshop guide. The sea breeze blew. The weather vane turned, and its loose metal edge went tap, tap, tap against the wooden post. Then came a pause.

Deniz looked. Fresh scratches were beside the metal edge.

“Watch again,” said the guide.

A breeze came. The vane turned. Tap, tap, tap. The scratches showed the touching place.

The guide safely held the vane still. No taps came.

Deniz stayed on the ground. “It needs an adult to fix,” said the guide. The sound had a source, but the next safe choice was still waiting.

Back at the workshop, Deniz told the guide, “The wind moves the vane. Its edge touches the post. The scratches show where. Then come three taps.”

“Should I climb and fix it?” Deniz asked.

“No,” said the guide. “We will do it safely.”

The guide fitted a small spacer at the worn place, keeping the loose edge from hitting the post. They watched the vane.

A breeze came. The vane turned. No taps.

Another breeze came. Still no taps.

A stronger breeze moved the vane, but the post stayed quiet.

Deniz smiled. The careful choice was clear: ask for help, make one safe change, and check what happened.

Later, Deniz stood in the public garden. Wind rustled the leaves and waved the flag. The sea rolled and shimmered. Deniz listened, but no three taps came from the old observation point.

At the library, Deniz told the librarian, “The wind moved the vane. The loose edge touched the post. The scratches showed where. The workshop fixed it with a spacer. Then the taps stopped.”

The librarian smiled. Deniz felt calm and proud. Watching, checking, and asking the workshop guide for safe help had turned a strange sound into something clear.
