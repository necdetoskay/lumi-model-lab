The Space Between Words

Deniz skipped into the familiar community room, holding a new picture. It had a bright red sun and three blue birds. “Mira! Look!” Deniz held it up. Mira carried wet cups and paintbrushes. She glanced over. “Not now,” she said, and turned toward the sink. She set Deniz’s picture face-down beside the supplies. Deniz’s smile slipped away. Deniz felt small, sad, and unsure, and sat near the art table instead of joining happily.

Deniz sat near the art table and held their hands tight. “I feel sad,” Deniz whispered. “Maybe Mira does not like my picture.” Mira turned, carefully slid the picture away from the wet cups and brushes, and left it on a clean, dry spot.

Deniz looked at the red sun. If Mira did not like it, why keep it safe? Deniz felt sad. “I will look and listen,” Deniz said softly. “Then I can find out.”

Deniz held the picture and watched. Mira lifted it from the dry spot and carried it to a dry shelf. Then she carried the paint water and paper towels to the sink.

The cups clinked. Water swished. Mira checked the clock. “First clean, then pictures,” she said softly.

Deniz looked at the red sun and the three blue birds. Mira was keeping the picture dry. She was busy cleaning, too.

But Deniz still felt sad. Did “Not now” mean never? Or did it mean later? Deniz hugged the picture. Looking and listening had helped, but Deniz still needed to ask Mira.

The last cup was dry. Mira put the brushes away. The room smelled like soap and paint. Deniz could see the picture on the dry shelf. Red sun. Three blue birds.

Deniz touched the paper. “I felt sad when you said, ‘Not now.’ Did you mean later?”

Deniz whispered it once. Then again, softer. Deniz did not say, “You do not like me.” That was a guess, and Deniz wanted to ask.

Mira was by the art table, folding the towels. Deniz picked up the picture and walked over. Deniz stood beside Mira, holding the picture.

Mira looked at Deniz. “Yes,” she said. “I meant not now—not while the paint was wet. I wanted to see your picture after I cleaned up.”

Deniz held the paper close. “I thought you did not want it.”

Mira’s face became soft. “I’m sorry. I turned away, and I did not tell you.”

Deniz looked at the red sun and three blue birds. Then Deniz looked at Mira. “It is okay. You can see it now.”

Together, they carried the picture to the community room’s picture shelf. Mira helped place it where everyone could see. “We can look at it after cleanup,” she said.

“Together?” Deniz asked.

“Together,” said Mira.

After cleanup, Mira and Deniz stood by the picture shelf. Mira pointed to the bright red sun.

“I like the warm sun,” she said. “And I like the three blue birds.”

Deniz smiled. The picture was safe, and Mira was looking at it with Deniz.

Mira picked up a brush and turned to put it away. Deniz remembered that “Not now” had meant “later,” not “never.” This time, Deniz did not guess.

“Later?” Deniz asked.

Mira turned back. “Yes, later.”

Deniz held Mira’s hand. Together, they stayed in the bright, busy room.
