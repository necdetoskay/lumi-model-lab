The Quiet Signal

Deniz was four and liked to listen. In the quiet library by the sea, Deniz sat near the window. Outside, the coastal town glittered, and the old observation point stood on the hill.

Tap. Tap. Tap.

Then silence.

Deniz looked up. After a little while: tap, tap, tap.

“Again!” Deniz whispered.

Deniz drew three dots. “What is it?”

The librarian listened too. “Let’s listen carefully,” she said. “We need not guess yet.”

Deniz left the library and went with the librarian to the public garden. The path stayed safe and open. Near the flower beds, Deniz heard, “Tap, tap, tap,” then waited. The sea wind bent the grass in shiny waves. On the hill, the little vane atop the old observation point turned. Tap, tap, tap—and the vane turned again. Deniz pointed, then added a wiggly line beside the three dots. “The wind moves it,” Deniz said. “Maybe. But is it the taps? I don’t know yet.”

At the workshop, Deniz held up the drawing. “Three taps,” Deniz said. “The vane moved too.”

The craftsperson looked at an old repair card. “I have seen that vane,” she said. “Its matching loose brass flap can tap the post when wind pushes it.”

She gave Deniz a bright ribbon. “Take it with you. It will show which way the wind blows and how hard.”

Deniz watched the ribbon flutter. “So the flap may make the sound,” Deniz said. “But sound is not its meaning yet.”

The librarian nodded. “We can watch next.”

The librarian walked with Deniz to the old observation point. Deniz held the bright ribbon.

Whoosh! The ribbon lifted. The vane turned. Its loose brass flap tapped the post. Tap, tap, tap.

Deniz’s eyes grew wide. “It really does it!”

The wind grew stronger. The ribbon pulled hard, and the three taps came again. Below the post, the steps were wet. A simple line marked the edge.

Deniz stayed beside the librarian. “The strong wind makes the taps,” Deniz said. “But what should we do when they come again?” Deniz looked at the wet steps and the line.

Deniz looked at each clue. The bright ribbon pulled. The vane turned. The loose flap tapped: tap, tap, tap. The steps were still wet, and the line was still between Deniz and the post.

Deniz held the librarian’s hand. “Three taps mean strong sea wind,” Deniz said. “We stay here. Please secure the gate.”

The librarian nodded. “Good noticing.” Together they stayed behind the line. The librarian closed the gate and fastened its latch. No one climbed the wet steps.

Deniz looked once more at the ribbon, vane, and flap. They moved together again. The taps were not a call for help. They told people to keep back when the sea wind blew.

At the workshop, the craftsperson tightened the loose brass flap on the old observation point’s vane. Now it could tap safely when the sea wind blew.

In the library, the librarian made a clear picture note: three dots, a wiggly wind line, and the words, “Strong sea wind.” The note showed the same three dots Deniz had drawn.

In the public garden, the vane turned. Tap, tap, tap. The ribbon lifted softly. Deniz pointed to the note. “Three taps mean strong sea wind,” Deniz said. The gate stayed shut, and everyone knew to stay behind the line.
