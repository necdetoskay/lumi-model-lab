The Quiet Signal

Deniz walked with Auntie along the seaside path. They stopped behind the safe rail at the old observation point. From the gate came three soft taps. Tap, tap, tap. Then it was quiet. Deniz listened. Tap, tap, tap! The same sound came again. Deniz stayed beside Auntie and looked at the gate. “What is making that sound?” Deniz asked. “I want to find out, not guess.”

Deniz and Auntie walked to the public garden beside the path. A sea breeze came. Whoosh! The grass bent low. Deniz watched. Soon, from the old observation point, came three soft taps. Tap, tap, tap.

The breeze faded. The grass stood tall again. The taps stopped.

After a while, the breeze came back. The grass bent. Then came three taps again.

Deniz and Auntie went back behind the safe rail. Deniz pointed toward the gate. “Could the wind matter?” Deniz asked. “I still cannot see which part moves.”

Deniz and Auntie went to the library. On a low table, Deniz looked at a simple town map. It showed the blue sea, the garden, and the old observation point. A tiny gate stood at the point’s edge. Beside the map, an old picture showed the same gate, with rails and sea behind it. “That is our gate,” Deniz said.

The librarian pointed to a sign. “Keep the gate latched,” she read.

Deniz remembered the breeze and the three taps. “Could a loose latch make the taps?” Deniz asked. Then Deniz nodded. “We will look from behind the safe rail.”

At the town workshop, Deniz and Auntie watched a helper work at a small wooden board. On the board was a loose metal latch. “I will not touch it,” said Deniz.

The helper gently moved air past the latch with a flat fan. Tap, tap, tap. The sound was soft and just like the three taps at the observation point.

Deniz looked closely. “Moving air can make a loose latch tap,” Deniz said. “But this is only a model. We need to watch the real gate.”

Auntie smiled. “From behind the safe rail.”

Deniz nodded. “We will watch together.”

Deniz and Auntie stood behind the safe rail at the old observation point. A sea breeze came from the water. The garden grass bent. At the same moment, the gate latch lifted and fell. Tap, tap, tap!

Deniz watched it happen again. The breeze moved the latch, and the latch made the three taps.

Deniz pointed. “The wind taps the loose latch. The gate can open. Please help me secure it.”

Auntie nodded. Deniz stayed behind the rail while Auntie carefully fastened the latch. Then Auntie checked it once more. It held tight.

Deniz looked at the quiet gate. The workshop model had matched the real gate.

Another sea breeze swept in from the water. The garden grass bent and danced. Deniz watched the gate.

It stayed still. No tap, tap, tap. The latch held tight.

Deniz smiled. “The breeze came back, but the taps did not. Auntie fixed the loose latch. I watched the grass, the latch, and listened. Now I understand.”

Auntie hugged Deniz. “You watched carefully,” she said.

Deniz looked at the quiet gate. The sea breeze blew again, but the gate stayed safely latched and silent.
