The Quiet Signal

On a breezy afternoon, four-year-old Deniz sat with Auntie Mira in the town library. A coastal map lay open.

“Tap, tap, tap.”

Deniz looked up. The quiet sound came from outside. Then it came again.

“Tap, tap, tap!”

“Again!” said Deniz.

The map showed the old observation point by the sea, the public garden, and the workshop.

“What makes it?” Deniz asked.

“We can listen, look, and ask,” said Auntie.

Deniz pointed. “Let’s find out safely. No guessing.”

Deniz and Auntie Mira walked to the public garden. They stayed together on the path.

First, Deniz felt the sea breeze. The tall sea grass bent and swished.

Then came the sound. “Tap, tap, tap.”

Deniz watched the grass bend just before the taps. A little garden sign twitched too. Did it make the sound? Deniz was not sure.

Deniz made three taps with one finger on a bench. “Tap, tap, tap. I will listen and look.” Auntie Mira smiled. They stayed side by side.

At the town workshop, Deniz stayed beside Auntie Mira. Deniz kept hands away from the tools. A thin metal strip hung near a wooden edge. A little breeze moved it.

“Tap, tap, tap!”

Deniz listened closely. The sound was like the taps at the library and in the garden. But was it the same sound?

On the wall, a repair drawing showed the old observation point. A loose strip was drawn on its railing. Deniz looked at the drawing, then at Auntie Mira.

“Can we go see it? Together?”

“Yes,” said Auntie Mira. “We will look safely.”

Together, Deniz and Auntie Mira walked to the old observation point. From the safe path, Deniz saw a thin metal strip hanging from the railing. A sea breeze moved it. It touched the wood.

“Tap, tap, tap!”

Deniz looked at the workshop drawing. The strip was in the same place. Auntie Mira gently held her coat between the breeze and the strip. The strip stopped moving. No taps came.

She lowered the coat. The breeze returned. The strip touched the railing again.

“Tap, tap, tap!”

Deniz watched closely. “It might be the sound,” Deniz said. “I want to check once more.”

The sea breeze came back and lifted the strip.

“Three taps next,” Deniz said.

The strip swung and touched the railing.

Tap, tap, tap.

Deniz watched closely. “I know now,” Deniz said. “The breeze moves the strip. The strip touches the railing. That makes three taps. It is not a person calling us.”

Auntie Mira smiled. “What should we do?”

“Tell the workshop,” said Deniz. “They can repair it. I must not touch it.”

Auntie Mira nodded. Together, they stayed on the safe path. Deniz looked once more at the loose strip. The mystery was clear, and the next step was safe: tell an adult who could fix it.

The workshop workers secured the loose strip. Deniz watched from the safe path with Auntie Mira. The sea breeze moved the railing, but no taps came.

At the library, Deniz held up one finger, then tapped three times. “The breeze moved the strip,” Deniz said. “The strip touched the railing. Tap, tap, tap!”

Auntie Mira smiled. “You looked and listened carefully.”

Deniz smiled too. The sound was quiet now. The town still had its sea breeze, its railing, and its old observation point—but the three taps were gone.
