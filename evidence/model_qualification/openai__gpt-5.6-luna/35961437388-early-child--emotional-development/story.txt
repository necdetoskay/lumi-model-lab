The Space Between Words

Deniz skipped into the neighborhood library with a card held in both hands. The card was blue, with a big yellow star on the front. Deniz had made it for Lila.

The story room smelled like books and crayons. Ms. Jo, the librarian, smiled from the little desk. Lila sat on the rug, waiting for story time.

“Lila!” Deniz called.

Usually, Lila jumped up. Usually, she said, “Deniz!” and they chose a book together.

Today, Lila looked up but did not smile.

“I made this for you,” Deniz said, holding out the card.

Lila took it very quickly. Then she turned her body away. She held the card close and whispered something to Ms. Jo.

Deniz stood still. The card had been warm in Deniz’s hands. Now those hands hung by Deniz’s sides.

“Do you want to play with the farm?” Deniz asked.

Lila looked at the rug. She did not answer.

Deniz’s tummy felt tight. Lila was acting different. Maybe she did not like the card. Maybe she did not like Deniz.

Deniz looked at Ms. Jo. Ms. Jo was putting out the story books, just as she always did. Lila was still holding the card, but she kept it turned away.

Deniz wanted to run to the reading corner and hide. But Deniz stayed. Leaving would not tell Deniz what had happened. Saying, “You are mean!” would not tell Deniz either.

Deniz took a slow breath.

“I want to know,” Deniz whispered.

So Deniz sat on the edge of the rug and watched. The story room was ready for a story, but Deniz’s eyes stayed on Lila and the quiet card.

Deniz stayed on the edge of the rug. The farm animals were near the craft table, so Deniz walked over to get the little brown horse.

There, beside the crayons, Deniz saw the blue card.

One corner was bent down. A small, sticky-looking juice mark shone on the front, near the yellow star.

Deniz touched the corner with one finger. It did not spring back.

“Oh,” Deniz said softly.

Lila was standing by Ms. Jo’s desk. She held the card against her tummy.

“Do you have tape?” Lila asked.

Ms. Jo opened a drawer. “Here is some.”

Lila took the tape. She looked at the card, then at Deniz.

Deniz’s eyes met hers.

Quickly, Lila turned around and tucked the card behind her back.

The tight feeling came back to Deniz’s tummy.

Deniz knew some things. Deniz had seen the bent corner. Deniz had seen the juice mark. Deniz had heard Lila ask for tape. Those were things Deniz really saw and heard.

But Deniz had also made a guess.

Maybe Lila did not want to play with Deniz.

The guess felt heavy. It sat beside the facts, but it was not the same as a fact.

Deniz watched Lila pull a long piece of tape from the roll. Lila tried to put it on the card, but the tape stuck to her finger. She frowned and rubbed it off.

“Do you want the farm?” Deniz asked.

Lila pressed the card flat against her shirt. She did not answer.

Deniz looked down at the little brown horse. Lila was still hiding the card. That made the hurtful guess feel possible.

But the bent corner and the tape made another guess possible too.

Maybe Lila was worried about the card.

Deniz did not know which guess was right. So Deniz stayed near the craft table, watching quietly and waiting to learn more.

Deniz stayed by the craft table.

Deniz looked at the card again. The blue card lay near a small paper cup. A little juice was still at the bottom of the cup. The bent corner was right beside it.

Deniz looked at the cup. Then Deniz looked at Lila.

The cup was a clue. The bent corner was a clue. But clues did not tell the whole story.

Deniz walked to Ms. Jo’s desk.

“Ms. Jo?” Deniz asked.

“Yes?”

“Can cards get bent by accident?”

Ms. Jo looked at Deniz. She did not look at Lila. “What makes you wonder?”

Deniz pointed to the card. “It has a bent corner. And there is juice.”

Ms. Jo nodded slowly. “You noticed two things.”

Deniz waited. Ms. Jo did not say what the things meant. She only gave a small, thoughtful hum.

Behind them, Lila stopped moving.

Deniz turned around. Lila had heard the question. She held the card with both hands and hugged it close to her chest.

“Not yet,” Lila said.

Her voice was very small.

Deniz blinked. “Not yet what?”

Lila looked down at the card. Her cheeks looked warm. She shook her head and held the card tighter.

Deniz felt the sore place in the tummy feeling. Lila was still not answering. Lila was still keeping the card away.

But now Deniz remembered the cup. Deniz remembered the bent corner. Deniz remembered Lila asking for tape.

Maybe Lila did not want to play.

Maybe Lila was worried about the card.

Deniz still did not know.

Deniz could have said, “You do not like me.” But those words felt sharp, and Deniz did not know if they were true.

So Deniz took one slow breath and touched a crayon.

“Can I sit here?” Deniz asked.

Lila looked up. She did not answer, but she moved one crayon aside. The space beside her was still small. It was not an invitation exactly.

It was not a door shut tight, either.

Deniz sat near the space and watched the card, the cup, and Lila’s quiet hands.

Deniz sat beside the little space Lila had made.

The blue card was between them. Its corner still bent. The juice cup still sat near the crayons.

Deniz looked at Lila’s quiet hands. Then Deniz looked at Ms. Jo.

“Ms. Jo,” Deniz said, “I saw Lila take my card. I felt sad. I thought she did not want me.”

Ms. Jo came closer and knelt beside Deniz.

“You felt sad,” she said.

Deniz nodded. “And I saw her hide it.”

“You saw Lila take the card and hide it,” said Ms. Jo. “Those are things you saw.”

Deniz touched the bent corner with one finger. “The card is hurt, too.”

Ms. Jo gave a small nod. She did not tell Deniz what Lila meant. She did not tell Deniz what had happened.

“What can you ask Lila?” Ms. Jo asked.

Deniz looked at Lila. Lila was still looking down.

Deniz tried the words softly. “What happened?”

Ms. Jo smiled a little. “That is a clear question.”

Deniz said it again, just to hear it. “What happened?”

The words felt small. They also felt less sharp than, “You do not want me.”

Lila stood up.

She walked to Ms. Jo’s desk and came back holding a little roll of tape. She held it out toward Deniz. Then she stopped. The tape hung between her fingers.

Lila looked at Deniz and waited.

Deniz looked at the tape. Deniz looked at the bent blue card. Deniz remembered Lila’s quiet voice.

Deniz felt sad. Deniz felt worried. But Deniz did not know the whole story yet.

Deniz took one slow breath.

“I saw you take my card,” Deniz said. “I felt sad. I thought you did not want me.”

Lila’s fingers squeezed the tape.

Deniz’s tummy felt wiggly. The question was ready, but saying it felt hard.

Ms. Jo stayed quiet beside them.

Deniz looked at Lila’s face instead of looking away.

“What happened?” Deniz asked.

Lila held the tape close to the card. She did not answer yet.

The bent corner rested between the two children. So did the question.

Lila looked at Deniz’s face. Her fingers stayed tight around the tape.

“Did the card bend?” Deniz asked.

Lila nodded. “Yes.”

Her voice was very small.

“Juice bent it,” she said. “I reached for the crayons. My cup went bump.”

Deniz looked at the cup beside the crayons. Then Deniz looked at the bent blue corner.

“Oh,” Deniz said.

Lila held the card against her shirt. “I hid it because I thought you would be angry.”

Deniz touched their tummy. It still felt wiggly. “I was sad when you did not answer me.”

“I was worried,” Lila said. “I wanted to fix it first.”

Deniz looked at the card again. Lila had taken it away. Lila had not answered. Those things had felt like, “Go away.” But Lila’s hands had also held the card very carefully. She had asked for tape. The bent corner and the tape were not a secret message about Deniz. They were part of Lila’s worry.

“You were worried about the card,” Deniz said.

Lila nodded.

“And I was worried about you,” Deniz said.

Lila’s shoulders loosened a little. “I still want to play.”

“Me too,” said Deniz.

Ms. Jo slid a smooth piece of paper across the table. “You can work together here.”

Deniz and Lila put the card flat on the table. Lila pressed the bent corner down. Deniz held it still while Lila placed a strip of tape over it.

The tape made a shiny line across the blue paper.

“It looks like a bridge,” Deniz said.

Lila smiled. “A card bridge.”

Ms. Jo gave them a small yellow paper star. Deniz put glue on the back. Lila pressed the star over the taped corner.

The card had a new star beside the old one. Its corner was not exactly the same, but it stayed flat.

Deniz picked it up carefully. “It is fixed with us.”

Lila leaned close. “Can we play now?”

Deniz nodded. “Yes. Let’s choose a book.”

Together, they carried the repaired card to the story rug. The tape, the stars, and the little juice mark were still there. So was Lila. So was Deniz. This time, they walked side by side.

Deniz and Lila reached the story rug. Deniz placed the repaired card beside their storybook.

The card lay flat. The tape shone across the corner. Two yellow stars smiled from the blue paper.

Lila opened the book. “You choose the first picture,” she said.

Deniz looked at the page. It showed a big red bus, a little dog, and three round windows.

“The dog,” Deniz said.

Lila made a tiny dog voice. “Woof! I am riding the bus!”

Deniz laughed. “I am riding too!”

Ms. Jo sat nearby with a basket of books. She turned a page in her own book while the children made the bus go down the rug.

Then Lila stopped. She looked toward the craft table.

“I need crayons,” she said.

She stood and walked to the table. Deniz watched her pick up the red and yellow crayons. For one moment, Lila’s back was turned.

Deniz’s tummy gave one small wiggle.

Before, Deniz might have thought, She does not want me.

But Deniz looked at the crayons. Lila was choosing colors. She was not taking the card away.

“Are you making something?” Deniz asked.

Lila turned around. “Yes. I will tell you.”

She came back with the crayons and sat beside Deniz.

“I am making a bus for the dog,” she said. “And a bus for you.”

“And one for you,” said Deniz.

Lila drew a long yellow bus. Deniz drew a red one. Their crayons made soft scratchy sounds on the paper.

The repaired card stayed beside the book. When Deniz looked at it, Deniz saw the shiny tape and the new star. Lila had helped put them there. Deniz had helped too.

Ms. Jo smiled from her chair as the buses grew wheels and windows.

“Where will the buses go?” she asked.

“To the moon!” said Lila.

“To the library!” said Deniz.

The two buses rolled across the page, side by side, while the story room hummed softly around them.
