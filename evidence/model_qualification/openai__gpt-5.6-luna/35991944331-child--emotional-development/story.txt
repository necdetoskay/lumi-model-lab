The Space Between Words

The community room smelled like crayons and lemon cleaner. Deniz knew every table and every bright sign on the walls. On Saturday, the room would hold an open house. Today, Deniz and Noor were finishing their welcome map.

Deniz wrote neat labels: ART, KITCHEN, and QUIET CORNER. Noor drew blue arrows to show visitors where to go.

“Should this arrow point to the big door?” Deniz asked.

Noor checked the room. “Yes. People might miss it.”

They had one rule: choose map changes together.

When Deniz went to get a ruler, Noor lifted the map to move it away from a glue bottle. One corner caught under the bottle. Rrrip!

Noor froze. A small piece of the corner hung loose.

“Oh no,” Noor whispered.

Noor folded the map so the torn part was inside, then hurried to Ms. Imani’s desk.

“Can we find some tape?” Noor asked. “Before Deniz sees?”

Ms. Imani opened a drawer. “There may be some in the supply box.”

Deniz came back with the ruler. Noor was standing beside the blue folder, holding the folded map close.

“What are you doing?” Deniz asked.

Noor tucked the map nearer to the folder. “Nothing.”

Then Deniz heard Noor murmur, “Before Deniz sees.”

Deniz looked at the folded map. The rule about choosing together suddenly felt far away.

The next afternoon, Deniz came back to the community room. Noor was not at the map table yet.

Deniz stopped a few steps away. The map still had its folded corner. Beside it lay two tiny tape scraps. A pale, damp ring marked the table underneath. Noor’s blue folder sat close by.

“Noor does not want me on this project,” Deniz thought.

Then Deniz made a small pause. That was a guess. What had Deniz really seen? Noor had folded the map. Noor had said, “Before Deniz sees.” Noor had kept the blue folder nearby. Those were the facts.

Deniz’s chest felt tight. “I feel hurt,” Deniz whispered. “And worried.” Saying the feelings made them feel less like a giant, blurry cloud.

Noor hurried in and looked at the map. “Can I work alone for one minute?” Noor asked. “I need to do something first.”

Deniz looked at the folded corner. “Why?”

Noor rubbed the edge of the blue folder. “Because you might be upset. We were supposed to choose changes together.”

That sounded like more proof. Noor still would not show Deniz what was under the fold.

“Fine,” Deniz said, though it did not feel fine. Deniz carried the ruler to a reading table across the room. The map stayed between them, quiet and unfinished. Noor bent over it, and Deniz tried not to watch.

Deniz tried to read at the reading table, but the same thought kept bumping into the words.

Noor does not want me here.

Then Deniz glanced at the map. Noor had moved to get a pencil. The folded corner lifted a little. Under it, Deniz saw the pale ring on the table. It was right below the torn place, as if a cup had sat there.

Deniz looked at the tape scraps. A cup. A torn corner. Tape.

Maybe Noor had not ruined the map on purpose. Maybe Noor had tried to fix it.

The thought made Deniz feel less certain, but not less hurt. Noor had still hidden the map. Noor had still said, “Before Deniz sees.”

Deniz went to Ms. Imani’s desk. Ms. Imani was sorting colored paper into a tray.

“Ms. Imani, where did the wide tape go?” Deniz asked.

“In the supply drawer,” said Ms. Imani. “Noor asked me for some yesterday.”

“Did Noor say why?”

Ms. Imani shook her head. “No. Noor only asked for tape.” She pointed to the drawer and went back to sorting.

Deniz did not open it. The tape was not a secret anymore. Still, Ms. Imani had not answered the question Deniz really wanted to ask.

At the map table, Noor kept the blue folder close and pressed a pencil along one edge of the fold.

Deniz thought about the story in their head: Noor damaged the map and wanted Deniz gone. The damp ring and the tape fit another story: Noor made a mistake and was trying to repair it.

Both stories had pieces of truth. Deniz still needed to hear Noor’s story. But asking felt harder than finding tape.

Deniz sat at the reading table with a small piece of scrap paper. The map table was only a few steps away, but it felt very far.

Deniz wrote three questions:

What did I see?
How did I feel?
What happened?

Under the first question, Deniz wrote, “I saw Noor fold the map.” Under the second, Deniz wrote, “I felt hurt and worried.”

Deniz tapped the pencil. The last question was still empty.

“Maybe I can ask,” Deniz whispered.

Deniz tried one sentence. “You ruined the map.”

The words sounded sharp, so Deniz rubbed them out.

“I felt worried when I saw the folded map,” Deniz said softly. That sounded more like the truth. It told what Deniz felt without saying what Noor had meant.

Across the room, Noor gathered colored pencils into the blue folder. A strip of wide tape was pressed over one corner of the folded map. The tape did not make the map flat yet. It only held the loose piece in place.

Noor glanced toward Deniz, then looked down.

Deniz folded the scrap paper once. The note fit in one hand. Deniz walked to the map table but stopped a little way from it.

“Can we talk about the map?” Deniz asked. “Just us, here?”

Noor held the taped corner carefully. “Now?”

“After cleanup,” Deniz said. “I wrote down what I saw and how I felt. I want to ask what happened.”

Noor looked at the note, then at the folded map. For a moment, Noor’s fingers tightened around the blue folder.

“Okay,” Noor said. “After cleanup.”

Deniz nodded. The question was still hard, but now it had a place to begin.

After cleanup, Deniz and Noor sat at the map table. The folded map lay between them. Noor kept one hand on the taped corner.

Deniz held the scrap paper. “I saw you fold the map,” Deniz said. “I felt hurt and worried. I thought you did not want me on the project.”

Noor looked at the tape. “I did not mean that. The corner tore yesterday.”

“You tore it?”

“By accident,” Noor said. “I moved the map because there was a wet ring on the table. The corner caught on a cup. It made a rip.”

Deniz looked at the brown ring beneath the map. Then Deniz looked at the tape scraps. They fit Noor’s words.

“I heard you say, ‘Before Deniz sees,’” Deniz said. “I thought you were hiding the damage from me.”

Noor’s eyebrows lifted. “I was hiding it until the repair was finished. I thought you might be angry because we are supposed to choose changes together.”

Deniz felt the hurt still sitting inside, but it was smaller now. “I was angry,” Deniz said. “And worried. But I guessed what you meant.”

“I guessed what you would do,” Noor said. “I should have asked you to help.”

Deniz touched the edge of the map. “Next time, can we ask before changing it?”

Noor nodded. “Yes. Even if it is an accident.”

They unfolded the map slowly. The taped corner had held, but one side still curled up. Deniz smoothed it while Noor added another strip of tape. Then they pressed the map flat together, palm beside palm.

“There is one room left to label,” Noor said. “The quiet room.”

Deniz picked up the black marker. “Do you want it above the door drawing or beside it?”

“Beside it,” Noor said. “People may see the picture first.”

Deniz wrote QUIET ROOM in neat letters. Noor checked the spelling, then drew a small arrow toward the room.

The map was not perfect. A pale line still showed where the corner had torn. But it was flat, clear, and ready for Saturday.

Deniz folded the scrap paper and put it in the blue folder. Noor made space for it beside the colored pencils.

“See you at the open house?” Noor asked.

“See you,” Deniz said. This time, the words felt easy.

On Saturday, the community room buzzed with voices. Tables held storybooks, paper crafts, and cups of water. Near the door, Deniz and Noor stood beside their welcome map.

A family came in and looked at the map. Deniz noticed that the arrow near the quiet room was missing. Deniz picked up the black marker, then paused.

“Can I add an arrow here?” Deniz asked Noor.

Noor leaned close. “Maybe. That arrow might point visitors to the reading table instead. The quiet room is farther down the hall.”

Deniz looked at the map. The reading table was drawn near the first turn. Then Deniz checked the real hallway. The quiet room sign hung beside the second door.

“You are right,” Deniz said. “The arrow should point to the turn.”

“Let’s check from the doorway,” Noor said.

They walked three steps back. From there, the map made more sense. Deniz drew a small arrow toward the first turn. Noor added a second arrow near the quiet room. They stood back again.

“Now it is clear,” Deniz said.

A little boy pointed at the map. “Where are the books?”

“Right this way,” said Noor.

Deniz led him to the reading table. Soon, more visitors stopped to ask questions. Deniz and Noor took turns answering. When they were unsure, they looked at the map together.

At the end of the afternoon, the map was covered with tiny fingerprints, but every room could be found. The pale tear line still showed at the corner. Deniz saw it and smiled a little.

Noor folded the chairs. Deniz gathered the markers. They left the map flat by the door, ready to welcome the next visitor.
