The Missing Pattern

Deniz was seven and knew this learning room well. Maps covered one wall. Labeled objects stood on low shelves. A big activity table waited near the window.

On the table was a row of six route-card spaces. The labels read Door, Map, Objects, Schedule, blank, and Table. The card borders took turns: blue, yellow, blue, yellow, blue, yellow. But the fifth space was empty. Only its blue border showed.

Deniz looked at the schedule beside the cards. It said, “Map Path — 3:00.” That was soon.

“May I look for the missing card?” Deniz asked the guide.

“Yes,” said the guide. “You may check the labeled learning areas. I will stay right here.”

Deniz pointed to the empty space. “What picture belongs there?”

“I do not know yet,” said the guide. “The display was rearranged this morning. Extra route tools are in the labeled drawers.”

Deniz nodded. The drawers were low and easy to reach. Deniz could look safely without leaving the room.

First, Deniz touched the blue border around the empty space. Then Deniz looked at the cards on both sides.

“I need to find out before we choose a path,” Deniz said.

The guide smiled. “That is a good plan.”

Deniz took a small ruler from the table. First, Deniz laid it beside each card and looked at the borders.

“Blue, yellow, blue, yellow, blue, yellow,” Deniz said. “The blank space is blue, too.”

The guide nodded. “What does that tell you?”

“The missing card is in the blue route group,” Deniz said. “The yellow cards are for places.”

Then Deniz noticed a nearby activity sign. It showed a picture of stairs.

“Maybe the missing card shows stairs,” Deniz said. “The sign has stairs. Can we check that idea?”

“Yes,” said the guide. “What could we check?”

Deniz got down on one knee. “Maybe it fell under the table.”

Together, they looked under the activity table. Deniz checked the corners and felt along the floor. There was no card, no matching label, and no loose paper.

“Could it be behind a leg?” Deniz asked.

The guide helped move a chair, but nothing was there.

Deniz went back to the display and touched the empty space. It was clean. No dust mark showed where a card had slipped out. The blue border was not bent, either.

“If the card fell here, we might see a mark,” Deniz said. “But there is no mark.”

“So what might have happened?” asked the guide.

Deniz looked toward the low labeled drawers. “Maybe someone moved it when the display was changed.”

The stairs idea was still a guess. The missing card was probably somewhere else.

Deniz stood beside the schedule. A small note was taped under the three o'clock Map Path time.

“Choose a path everyone can use,” Deniz read slowly.

Deniz thought for a moment. “I know what ‘path’ means. What does ‘everyone’ mean here?”

The guide pointed to the wall map. “What do you think it means?”

Deniz looked at the map. It showed two lines from Door to Table. One line was labeled Stairs. The other was labeled Ramp.

“The stairs line is shorter,” Deniz said. “Maybe that is why the sign showed stairs.”

Deniz traced the other line with one finger. “But the ramp goes to the same places.”

The guide nodded. “What else do you notice?”

“There are no steps on the ramp line,” Deniz said. Then Deniz looked at the schedule again. “Some people may use wheels. Stairs would not work for them. The ramp would.”

The guide stayed quiet.

Deniz tapped the note. “So ‘everyone’ means the path should work for all the people in the group.”

“That is what I think,” said the guide. “What do you think now?”

Deniz looked back at the stairs picture on the nearby sign. “I thought the missing card might show stairs. But the schedule says to choose a path everyone can use. The ramp is a better guess now.”

Deniz checked the map again. Near the activity supplies was a label that said Tools.

“Tools,” Deniz read. “Could the moved card be there?”

“It could,” said the guide. “How can we check?”

Deniz pointed to the low drawers. “We can look in the drawer marked Tools.”

The ramp was still only a guess, but now it was a guess Deniz could test.

Deniz knelt beside the drawers. One front said TOOLS.

“Let us check this one,” said the guide.

Deniz pulled it open. Inside were tape, pencils, and the small ruler Deniz had used before. Deniz moved the pencils aside and looked under the tape.

A paper was stuck to the back of the drawer. Deniz read it aloud. “During the morning display change, route cards were put away with the tools.”

“So the card was moved,” Deniz said. “It was not lost under the table.”

“Look carefully,” said the guide.

Deniz searched between the tools. A card peeked out beside the ruler. It had a blue border. Its picture showed a ramp. A small arrow pointed along it.

Deniz carried the card to the display but did not put it in yet.

First, Deniz looked at the empty space. It had a blue border too. The other borders went blue, yellow, blue, yellow. This card would fit the pattern.

Next, Deniz looked at the wall map. The arrow on the card pointed the same way as the line marked Ramp. The map’s label near the drawer said Tools. The card had been found in the Tools drawer, just as the note said.

The guide pointed to the empty space. “Tell me what you found before you place it.”

Deniz held the card with both hands. “It was in the Tools drawer because the display was changed. It has a blue border for the blue space. It shows the ramp, and its arrow matches the ramp line on the map.”

Deniz glanced at the schedule note. “The ramp might be the path everyone can use, too.”

The guide smiled. “You checked more than one clue.”

Deniz nodded, still holding the card. The pattern was ready, but the card was not in its place yet.

Deniz carried the ramp card back to the display. The empty space waited between Schedule and Table.

Deniz looked once more at the borders. Blue, yellow, blue, yellow, blue, yellow. The ramp card belonged in the blue space.

Slowly, Deniz slid it into place. It fit.

Now the six cards made a path from Door to Table. Deniz traced the pictures with one finger. The map, the objects, the schedule, the ramp, and the table all matched the display.

The guide pointed to the schedule note. “What does it say?”

Deniz read, “Choose a path everyone can use.”

Deniz looked at the wall map. The stairs line was shorter. The ramp line was longer, but it went from the same Door to the same Table. It had no steps.

Then Deniz looked at the Tools drawer. The small note still showed why the card had been there. The display had been changed that morning, so the route card had been put away with the tools.

Deniz touched the blue border. “The pattern says it goes here. The map says it is the ramp. The schedule says everyone can use the path. The card was in the Tools drawer because of the note.”

The guide nodded. “Which path will you choose?”

Deniz stood tall and looked at both lines on the map.

“The ramp is better because everyone can use it. Some people cannot use stairs.”

“The stairs are shorter,” said the guide. “Is it wrong to have stairs?”

Deniz shook their head. “No. Stairs can work for some people. But the ramp can work for everyone.”

The guide smiled. “You checked the pattern, the map, the schedule, and the drawer. Your choice comes from all four clues.”

Deniz pressed the ramp card flat. The Map Path display was ready for three o’clock. The longer line was the better line for the whole group.

At three o’clock, the Map Path session began.

The guide stood by the Door card. A small group gathered around the display. The guide held the map and led everyone along the ramp. The ramp line went from Door to Table, just as the card showed.

Deniz walked with the group. In one hand, Deniz held a pencil. At each stop, Deniz traced the matching card: Door, Map, Objects, Schedule, Ramp, Table.

When a learner looked unsure, Deniz pointed to the label on the wall. “Here is Map,” Deniz said. Then the guide led the group to the map.

At the ramp, everyone could follow the same path. No one had to split away. The route was longer than the stairs, but the whole group reached the Table together.

The guide placed the map beside the finished cards. “Thank you for checking the pattern,” the guide said. “How did you choose?”

Deniz looked at the blue and yellow borders. Then Deniz looked at the map and the schedule.

“I tested it,” Deniz said. “The pattern fit the ramp card. The map showed the ramp. The schedule said everyone could use it. Guessing stairs would not have worked as well.”

The guide nodded. “You checked before you chose.”

Deniz smiled and tapped the Ramp card with the pencil. The labels, the map, and the cards all pointed the same way. The activity was ready, and everyone could follow the path together.
