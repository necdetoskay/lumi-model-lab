The Space Between Words

On Wednesday afternoon, Deniz hurried into the neighborhood library. The makers’ corner smelled like paper, glue, and warm air from the little fan. Deniz came here every week to build things with Mira while Mr. Kemal helped nearby.

“Rocket day!” Deniz said.

Their paper rocket stood on the table. Its blue fins were shiny with wet glue. Mira was placing scraps into a box when Mr. Kemal said, “Mira, could you clear this table before the next group comes?”

“Okay,” said Mira.

She carefully lifted Deniz’s rocket and carried it to a shelf by the window. Then she went back to the table.

Deniz reached for a strip of silver paper. Mira quickly covered a small tray with a cloth.

“Deniz should not see it yet,” Mira whispered.

She turned away.

Deniz’s hand stopped in the air. The words felt like a door closing. Maybe Mira was making something for the library display without Deniz. Maybe their shared rocket was not really shared anymore.

Deniz looked at the rocket drying alone by the window. Their chest felt tight and hot. Without saying goodbye, Deniz picked up their backpack and walked out of the makers’ corner, carrying a hurt feeling and a very big guess.

The next afternoon, Deniz came back to the library makers’ corner. Mira was there, working near the covered tray. She was cutting paper stars while the rocket rested on the shelf by the window.

Deniz wanted to walk over. Instead, Deniz took a chair at a quiet table. They opened their notebook, but the words on the page would not stay still.

“I feel left out,” Deniz whispered.

The feeling had a name now. It still felt heavy, but naming it made a little space around it.

Deniz drew two columns. On one side, Deniz wrote what really happened:

Mira moved the wet rocket.
Mira covered the tray.
Mira whispered, “Deniz should not see it yet.”
Mira turned away.

Deniz looked at the other side. Under Guess, Deniz wrote: Mira does not want me in the shared display.

Deniz stared at that sentence. It felt true, like a pebble in a shoe. But it was still a guess. The first side held things Deniz had seen and heard. The second side held what Deniz thought those things meant.

Mira glanced over. “Do you want to help cut fins?” she asked.

Deniz shook their head, but not sharply. “Not yet. I’m going to work here.”

Mira nodded and returned to the paper stars.

Deniz touched the pencil marks. Maybe the guess was right. Maybe it was not. The rocket had been wet, and the tray was still covered. Deniz needed to know more before deciding what Mira meant.

Deniz’s pencil stopped. The guess still sat in the notebook like a pebble.

Mr. Kemal was putting scissors into a blue basket. Deniz carried the notebook over.

“Mr. Kemal, where should wet projects go?” Deniz asked.

He pointed to the shelf by the window. “There. Glue and paint need time to dry. If we move a wet project too soon, it can stick to the table or bend.”

Deniz looked at the rocket. Its paper sides had curled a little, but the nose was safe.

“So Mira moved it because it was wet?” Deniz asked.

“That would make sense,” said Mr. Kemal. “I asked her to clear the table. She was also helping me get things ready for the Saturday display.”

Deniz glanced toward Mira. She was still cutting small paper shapes beside the covered tray. Her scissors made a soft snip, snip, snip.

“Did you hear what she said?” Deniz asked.

Mr. Kemal shook his head. “No. You will need to ask Mira about her words.”

Deniz returned to the quiet table. In the notebook, Deniz added a new line under Facts: The drying shelf protects wet glue and paint. Mira was helping prepare the display.

The rocket’s move had an answer now. It did not look like proof that Mira wanted Deniz gone. But the covered tray and those words still made Deniz’s chest feel tight.

One explanation fit some of the facts. It did not explain all of them.

Deniz closed the notebook. Asking Mira still felt hard, but guessing alone felt worse.

Deniz closed the notebook, then opened it again.

On the wall beside the makers’ corner was the Saturday display list. Deniz read the names slowly. Near the bottom it said:

“Paper Rockets: Deniz and Mira.”

Deniz touched the words. Their rocket still had both names. That did not prove everything was fine, but it did not look like being pushed out, either.

Mira was at the long table, sorting colored paper. Mr. Kemal was helping someone print a sign. The library was calm, with only the hum of the printer and the soft turn of pages nearby.

Deniz could ask now. Not while Mira was rushing. Not with a sharp voice. Deniz waited until Mira put down the scissors and took a drink of water.

Then Deniz whispered the words to the empty chair first.

“I saw you move my wet rocket. I saw you cover the tray. I heard you say I should not see it yet. I felt left out. What did you mean?”

Deniz frowned. “That sounds a little too big.”

They tried again, using the words in the notebook.

“When you moved the rocket and covered the tray, and I heard you say, ‘Deniz should not see it yet,’ I felt left out. Can you tell me what you meant?”

That sounded right. It told what happened. It told the feeling. It asked instead of guessing.

Deniz folded the notebook and stood. The rocket was still on the drying shelf, safe and nearly ready. Mira looked up as Deniz came closer.

“Can I ask you something?” Deniz said.

Mira nodded.

Deniz took one slow breath and began.

“When you moved the rocket and covered the tray, and I heard you say I should not see it yet, I felt left out,” Deniz said. “What did you mean?”

Mira’s eyes grew round. She glanced at the drying shelf. “Oh! I did not mean you should not see the rocket. Mr. Kemal asked me to clear the table, so I moved it there because it was still wet.”

Deniz nodded. “I know that part now.”

Mira lifted the cloth from the tray. Inside were small gold and silver stars cut from shiny paper.

“I was making these for our rocket,” she said. “I told myself, ‘Deniz should not see it yet,’ because I wanted the stars to be a surprise. I should have said that more clearly.”

The stars sparkled like tiny windows in the light.

“I thought you were keeping our display from me,” Deniz said. “I’m sorry I guessed that without asking.”

“I’m sorry my words sounded that way,” Mira said. “I should have said, ‘Don’t look at the tray yet.’”

Mira picked up a star. “Want to help stick them on?”

“Yes,” Deniz said. “Can we put some on each side?”

“Good idea.”

They brought the dry rocket to the worktable. Deniz held it steady while Mira pressed a blue star near the nose. Then Deniz added a gold one beside it. The paper made a soft crinkle as they smoothed each label down.

They worked all around the rocket, choosing colors together. When they finished, Deniz lifted it carefully while Mira cleared a safe place beside the worktable.

“Ready to carry it over when it’s time?” Mira asked.

“Ready,” Deniz said.

They looked at the star-covered rocket together. Both names were still written on its base, and the paper sides were straight and dry. Deniz held one end, and Mira held the other, keeping it level as they prepared to take it to the display.

Deniz and Mira carried the rocket across the makers’ corner. They set it beneath the sign that said, “Saturday Community Display,” keeping it level between them.

Mr. Kemal looked up from a box of paper. “That looks ready.”

“Your drying shelf helped,” Deniz said. “The rocket stayed safe while the glue dried.”

“And the stars are a surprise,” Mira added. She pointed to the blue and gold labels shining along the sides. “We made them together.”

Mr. Kemal smiled. “Then it belongs right there.”

Deniz held the base while Mira adjusted the rocket’s nose. “A little to the left?” Deniz asked.

“Your left or my left?” Mira asked.

They both giggled. Deniz moved it a tiny bit. The rocket stood straight, with both names easy to see.

Mira reached toward a small card covered by a sheet of paper. Deniz watched her hand, then asked, “What is that?”

Mira stopped. “It is the card with the display title and our names. I covered it so the wet letters would not smudge.”

“Oh,” Deniz said. “Thanks for telling me.”

Mira lifted the cover. The card read: DENIZ AND MIRA’S STAR ROCKET.

Deniz grinned. “This time I asked before I guessed.”

“And I answered before you had to guess,” Mira said.

They looked at the rocket, then at each other. A guess could grow quite large in Deniz’s mind, like a balloon. But now the balloon had a little hole in it, and the air was slipping out.

Mr. Kemal placed the card beside the rocket. Deniz and Mira stood together to admire their display, already choosing which visitor they would show the shiny stars to first.
