The Space Between Words

At the community art table, Deniz made a paper rocket with Jo. Deniz came here often, and Jo always helped with tape and bright crayons. “Look!” Deniz said, holding up the rocket. Jo glanced over. “Later,” Jo said quickly. Jo put the rocket in a blue box, then hurried to clear cups and paper. Deniz watched Jo turn away. Deniz’s smile fell. Maybe Jo did not like the rocket.

Deniz’s hands felt heavy. Deniz looked at the rocket in the blue box. “I feel sad and left out,” Deniz whispered. “I think Jo does not like my rocket.” But a thought was not the same as a fact. Deniz stayed near the table and watched. Jo carried another picture over. Its paint was still shiny and wet. Jo placed that picture in the same blue box. Deniz blinked. Maybe the box had a job. Deniz still wished Jo had explained.

Deniz bent close to the blue box. On its side was a little sign. It showed a dripping paintbrush and said, “Wet pictures dry here.” Deniz looked at the rocket. Its paint still shone wet. The other picture was wet too. Both pictures could be drying. But Deniz remembered Jo saying, “Later,” then turning away so fast. The quick turn had felt unkind. Maybe Jo was helping the pictures. Maybe Jo still did not like Deniz’s rocket. Deniz hugged their hands together. The box gave a clue, but not the whole answer.

Deniz peered into the blue box. The rocket’s wet paint shone beside the other wet picture. Both pictures were drying. Maybe Jo had moved the rocket to help it. Still, Jo had said, “Later,” and turned away. Deniz’s chest felt tight. “I felt sad when you put my rocket away,” Deniz whispered. “Did you like it?” The words told what happened and how Deniz felt. They did not say, “You were mean.” Deniz took a small breath and held the question ready. Then Deniz walked toward Jo, ready to ask.

Jo looked up as Deniz came near. Deniz took a breath. “I felt sad when you put my rocket away. Did you like it?”

“I did,” said Jo. “I liked it a lot. The paint was wet, so I put it in the blue box to dry. I said ‘Later,’ but forgot to explain.”

Deniz looked at the bright rocket. “I thought you didn’t like it. My thought got mixed up.”

“I understand,” Jo said. “You felt sad.”

Together, they wrote DENIZ on a small label and placed it beside the rocket. “When it dries, we can put it on the picture wall,” said Jo.

Deniz smiled. “Can we make another picture?”

“Yes,” said Jo. “Together.”

At the next art time, Deniz saw the dry rocket on the picture wall. The small label beside it said DENIZ. Deniz touched the paper gently and smiled.

Jo sat beside Deniz at the art table. They made a bright sun picture together. Then Jo picked it up. “I need to move this wet picture to the blue box so it can dry,” Jo said.

“Okay,” said Deniz. “Thanks for telling me.”

Jo put the picture in the box, and Deniz chose a yellow crayon. Soon, they were making another picture together.
