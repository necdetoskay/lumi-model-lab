The Missing Pattern

Deniz entered the learning room. Maps, labels, and a picture schedule filled the walls and shelves. The pattern board showed a red circle, yellow square, red circle, yellow square, and an empty place. A blue triangle and a yellow square sat nearby. Deniz reached out but paused. “You may look, compare, and ask before moving anything,” said the guide. Paper and crayons waited in a tray. Deniz wanted to finish the pattern before the next activity.

Deniz pointed to the labels beside the board. “Circle, square, triangle,” Deniz read. Then Deniz said, “Circle, square, circle, square.” The shapes alternated, so the empty place needed a circle. Deniz picked up the blue triangle, then put it back. “Triangle is not next. The board does not support it.” The nearby yellow square did not fit either. Deniz searched, but no loose circle was available. Remembering the guide’s words, Deniz paused. “I need a circle,” Deniz said, “but I must check what it is for before moving anything.”

Deniz walked to the map. A blue route curled across it. Along the route sat a red circle and a yellow square. “A circle!” Deniz said. “I found one!” It looked just like the shape the pattern needed. Deniz reached toward it, then stopped. The circle was sitting on the map route, not loose nearby. The picture schedule showed a map first and a pattern next. “Map comes first. Pattern comes next,” Deniz said. “Maybe this circle goes on my board. Maybe it stays here.” Deniz looked closely and waited.

Deniz bent close to the map. A label beside it said, “Route markers stay on the map.” “Does this red circle stay here?” Deniz asked. “Yes,” said the guide. “Children will use the map soon.” Deniz looked at the picture schedule. Map came first. Pattern came next. Then Deniz looked back at the board in the distance. “The board goes circle, square, circle, square,” Deniz said. “It needs a circle, but this circle helps the map.” In the tools tray, labeled paper and crayons waited. Deniz picked up a paper and a red crayon. “I can make another circle,” Deniz said.

Deniz put the paper on the table and drew a red circle with the crayon. “Circle,” Deniz said, checking the circle label. Then Deniz faced the board. “Circle, square, circle, square,” Deniz repeated. “The next shape is circle.” Deniz remembered the red circle on the map. The map label said route markers stay on the map, and the guide had confirmed that children would use the route before the pattern activity. So the map circle had to remain there. Deniz placed the paper circle in the empty place and checked aloud: “Circle, square, circle, square, circle!” “I used the sequence and labels,” Deniz told the guide. “I left the map circle because it marks the route.” The guide smiled. “You checked instead of guessing.”

Soon it was Map time. The children followed the route, using the red circle that still marked it. Deniz watched the marker stay in place. After the map activity, it was Pattern time. At the board, the guide touched each card, and everyone said, “Circle, square, circle, square, circle.” The paper circle fit the empty place, so the pattern worked. Then the guide pointed back to the map. “Your circle stayed there because the map needed it.” Deniz smiled. “I looked, checked, and then chose.” Both learning tools were ready.
