The Space Between Words

On Tuesday afternoon, Deniz hurried into the community room with a glue stick in one hand. Mina was already spreading the welcome map across the big table.

“Look! The library card fits here,” Mina said.

Deniz smiled. They had worked happily on the map for three weeks. They had drawn streets, added a playground, and made tiny cards for places in the neighborhood. Now only a few spots were empty.

Ms. Lila set out colored paper near the display table. Deniz reached for the moon-shaped card they had painted blue and silver.

Then Mina leaned close to Ms. Lila. “Can you hide Deniz’s moon card in the supply box? And please don’t show Deniz the other thing yet.”

Deniz’s hand stopped.

Ms. Lila glanced at Mina. “All right,” she said.

Mina picked up the moon card. When Deniz took one step closer, Mina quickly put it in the box and snapped the lid shut.

A small worry curled inside Deniz. Why hide their card? What was the other thing? Deniz looked at the unfinished map. A moment ago, the table had felt warm and friendly. Now it felt as if a chair had been pulled away.

Mina began sorting paper, humming softly. Deniz said nothing. They did not know what Mina meant, but the hidden card and secret words felt hard to ignore.

The next afternoon, Deniz came back to the community room. Mina was at the map table, but Deniz chose a chair near the window.

Usually, Deniz would sit beside Mina and suggest a new street or a funny sign. Today, Deniz kept both hands in their lap. The map stayed spread out between them, with empty places waiting for cards.

Mina looked up. “I saved the blue marker for you.” She held it out.

“Thanks,” Deniz said, taking it.

The marker was Deniz’s favorite because it made smooth, dark lines. Mina remembered that. That should have made Deniz feel better. Instead, a worried thought poked again. Maybe Mina was being nice because she wanted Deniz to stop asking questions.

Mina bent over the map. “Do you think we need another street near the playground?”

Deniz looked at the empty space. “Maybe.”

Mina waited. “You always have good ideas.”

Deniz pressed the marker cap between their fingers. Mina sounded like Mina, the friend who had laughed with Deniz while drawing the map for three weeks. She was still saving markers and asking for ideas. But she had also hidden the moon card and the other thing.

Deniz felt hurt. Worried, too. Those feelings were real, but they did not tell Deniz exactly what Mina meant.

Still, Deniz began to think Mina was making a plan without them. The thought felt heavy, yet not certain. Deniz watched Mina quietly, wondering what else might fit—and whether there was a way to ask.

Deniz held the blue marker but did not draw. Mina was working at the far side of the map. She lifted a small white label and wrote carefully on it.

Deniz could see the words from their chair.

WELCOME TO OUR NEIGHBORHOOD
By Deniz and Mina

Deniz’s name was there beside Mina’s. Mina pressed the label flat near the library card, then checked the edges twice.

That did not look like a plan to leave Deniz out.

But a few minutes later, Mina opened the supply box just a crack. The blue-and-silver moon card was inside. It rested on top of a soft cloth, safe and clean. Mina tucked it back and closed the box.

The hidden card still made Deniz’s stomach feel twisty.

Ms. Lila came to the display table with a roll of tape. Mina spoke quietly to her. Deniz did not move closer. They could hear without peeking.

“It has to look right before Deniz sees it,” Mina said.

Before Deniz sees it.

Those words sounded secret again. Yet Mina had written Deniz’s name on the welcome label. She had saved the blue marker. She was taking care of the moon card.

Deniz made three quiet thoughts in their head.

I saw Mina hide the card.

I guessed she was making a plan without me.

I still need to ask what she means.

The guess felt big, but it was still a guess. Deniz looked at Mina, then at the unfinished map. They wanted to keep working together. They also wanted to know the truth.

Deniz put the marker down. Soon, they would ask, “I felt left out when I heard those words. What were you planning?”

Deniz looked at the unfinished map. The moon card was still in the closed supply box. Their question felt ready in their head, but their voice felt small.

Ms. Lila was sorting tape at the display table. Deniz walked over.

“Can I practice something?” Deniz asked.

“Of course,” said Ms. Lila.

Deniz took a breath. “I felt left out when I heard those words. What were you planning?”

Ms. Lila nodded. “That tells Mina what happened and how you felt. Then you can listen to her answer.”

Deniz rubbed the edge of the blue marker. “I almost want to say, ‘You are mean.’”

“You can tell her what she did,” said Ms. Lila, “but you do not have to give her a name. You heard her ask me to hide the moon card. You heard her say the map had to look right before you saw it. Those are the parts you know.”

Deniz glanced at the box. “I do not know the rest.”

“Right,” said Ms. Lila. “Mina can tell you that part.”

Ms. Lila did not open the box. She did not tell Deniz what Mina was doing. She only gave Deniz a little space near the map.

Mina was there, pressing the corner of the welcome label flat. She looked up when Deniz came closer.

“Do you want to add the playground?” Mina asked.

Deniz held the blue marker. Their fingers felt warm. The old worry bumped against the new words.

“Not yet,” Deniz said. “Mina, can we talk before we finish the display?”

Mina put down the label. “Okay.”

Deniz took one more breath and stood beside the map. The moon card stayed hidden, and the answer was still waiting between them.

Deniz stood beside the map and held the blue marker tightly.

“I heard you ask Ms. Lila to hide my moon card,” Deniz said. “Then you said the map had to look right before I saw it. I felt left out. What were you planning?”

Mina looked at the supply box. “I was making something for our map.”

She opened the box and took out the moon card. Under it was a silver paper cover shaped like a crescent moon. Tiny blue dots covered the silver paper.

“I used your moon card to make the shape,” Mina said. “I wanted it to be a special cover for the corner of our map. I kept it hidden so you would not see it before it was ready.”

Deniz touched the edge of the cover. It had a little fold that fit around the card.

“So you were not making a map without me?” Deniz asked.

“No,” Mina said. “I wanted it to be ours. But I did not tell you I was making a surprise. I just told you not to look. That was not a good way to do it.”

Deniz looked down at the blue marker. “I got scared instead of asking. Then I sat away and stopped helping.”

Mina nodded. “I noticed.”

For a moment, Deniz still felt a small knot in their chest. The secret had hurt, even though its reason was kind. Then Deniz held out the moon card.

“Can we put it in the cover now?”

Mina smiled. “Together.”

They slid the card into the silver crescent. Deniz added the library and playground cards. Mina filled the last empty space with the welcome label: BY DENIZ AND MINA. They pressed every corner flat and carried the finished map to the display table.

“What should we say next time?” Mina asked.

“Maybe, ‘I am planning a surprise. You can ask me about it,’” Deniz said.

“And you can ask instead of guessing,” Mina added.

Deniz looked at the shining moon in its new cover. “I can do that.”

The map was ready for the community room, with both their names and a moon they had fixed together.

At the next community-room meeting, Deniz and Mina stood by the display table. Their welcome map hung where everyone could see it. The silver moon shone in its special place.

Mina picked up the library card and paused.

“I am making one small surprise change,” she said. “It is for our map. I will tell you before I move anything else.”

Deniz watched the card in her hand. A tiny worry popped up, like a pebble in a shoe. Deniz took a breath.

“What kind of surprise change?” Deniz asked. “Is it something we can talk about?”

Mina smiled. “Yes. I want to add a little yellow path from the library to the playground. It shows the way families can walk between them.”

Deniz looked at the open space on the map. There was room for the path. Mina had not hidden a card or closed the supply box. She had told Deniz what she was doing.

“Can we choose the path together?” Deniz asked.

“Together,” Mina said.

Deniz held the yellow marker while Mina pointed. They drew a curving line past the park. Then they stepped back to look.

Ms. Lila came over with two pieces of tape. “That path will help visitors,” she said. “Would you like to hang the map a little lower?”

Deniz and Mina nodded. Together, they moved the map to a place where younger children could see the moon, the library, the playground, and the new yellow path.

Deniz smoothed the corner near the silver crescent. “The moon stays here,” Deniz said.

“Of course,” Mina replied. “It is our special part.”

A family entered the room and stopped to read the map. Deniz and Mina stood side by side. The map looked bright and welcoming, with both their names at the bottom and the moon shining above them.
