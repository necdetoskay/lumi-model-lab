# Fenerdeki Hırsız — Q7E six-scene candidate

## SC01

Ekin stood at the harbor wall, turning her grandfather’s brass compass between her hands. Beyond the boats, dark clouds gathered over the sea, but the water inside the harbor was still calm.

A sudden rush of wings whipped her hair across her face.

Something long and scaled swept over the wall. Before Ekin could close her fingers, the sea dragon snatched the compass from her hands. Brass flashed between its teeth.

“Hey!” Ekin scrambled onto the wall and raced along it after the dragon. “Give that back!”

The creature skimmed above the moored boats, then climbed toward the headland. Ekin jumped down onto the quay and kept running, dodging ropes and barrels without looking back.

“You won’t catch it that way,” someone called.

Ekin stopped only because the dragon was already pulling ahead. A girl she had never seen before stood nearby, pointing past the harbor. On the headland, the lighthouse rose against the darkening sky, its door open. The dragon was heading straight toward it.

“The lighthouse,” the stranger said. “That’s where it’s going.”

Ekin watched the dragon cross above the last boats, still carrying the compass. She hated that the girl was right.

“I know the coastal route,” the stranger added. “I’m Duru.”

“I didn’t ask for help.”

“No. But you need to reach the lighthouse.”

Ekin tightened her jacket and looked from Duru to the shrinking shape of the dragon. “Fine. We go together—but only until I get my compass back.”

Duru nodded. “Together, then.”

They turned toward the harbor road, ready to set out, while thunder muttered far across the water.

## SC02

Duru set off from the harbor and moved onto the coastal path. Ekin waited one stubborn heartbeat, then moved onto the coastal path after her.

Wind pressed at Ekin’s jacket as the harbor fell behind them. The path bent along the rising headland, and Duru kept ahead, guiding them along each turn without hesitation.

“Stay on this side,” Duru called over the wind. “The coastal route goes straight to the open lighthouse door.”

“I can see the path,” Ekin replied.

At the next bend, however, she started toward the other side. Duru stopped and pointed along the continuing coastal route. The lighthouse stood ahead, its open upper-room door facing the path.

Ekin looked from Duru’s choice to the doorway. Duru’s route was the right one, and Ekin knew it.

“Fine,” she said. “Lead the way. I’m following.”

They hurried on as thunder rolled nearer. Ekin kept searching the sky, but there was no sign of the sea dragon or the stolen compass. Every empty moment made her want to run faster.

Duru reached the lighthouse first. She moved through the open upper-room door and entered the lighthouse upper room.

Ekin reached the same doorway. She moved through it and entered the lighthouse upper room after Duru.

Now both girls stood inside the upper room. Ekin remained close to the entrance, guarded and impatient.

“You got me here,” she told Duru. “That doesn’t mean I trust you.”

Duru nodded. “Then don’t. Just keep going with me.”

Ekin glanced into the room. “Until I get my compass back.”

“Until then,” Duru agreed.

## SC03

Daylight filled the dry upper room. Across it, Ekin saw the sea dragon crouched beside the lighthouse nest, her broad body shining with seawater.

A small shape stirred within the nest. The dragon hatchling was there, no bigger than a cat. Its wings fluttered, but it stayed firmly on its feet, bright-eyed and unhurt.

The mother dragon shifted sideways. She planted herself across the approach to the nest, blocking Ekin’s path.

Then she lowered her head and placed the brass compass in the nest beside the hatchling.

Ekin’s breath caught. She saw the compass clearly, close enough for daylight to flash across its worn brass. She took one quick step forward, but Duru caught her attention with a sharp whisper.

“Wait. Look at the little one.”

Duru had noticed the hatchling. Ekin looked again and saw it watching the compass, then pressing close to its mother’s side.

“That doesn’t make the compass hers,” Ekin said.

“No,” Duru replied. “But the mother is guarding the way.”

Ekin searched the room for another approach. Beside the wall, both girls saw a narrow turning stair leading downward toward the lower maintenance room. Far below, visible through the stair opening, daylight and gray sea showed beyond a fixed iron grate.

Duru glanced from the stair back to the dragon. “Don’t rush her. We need to think.”

“My grandfather’s compass is right there.”

“And so is the dragon.”

Ekin kept her eyes on the brass circle in the nest. The mother remained across the approach, silent and unmoving. Impatience tightened Ekin’s hands inside her jacket sleeves.

“You can think,” she muttered. “I’m not leaving without it.”

## SC04

Thunder cracked over the lighthouse, and rain hammered the walls. The hatchling flinched against its mother.

The sea dragon turned toward it. Her broad body swung away from the nest approach, leaving a brief opening.

Ekin rushed through the open approach.

“Ekin, wait!” Duru shouted.

Ekin reached for the brass compass. Duru saw her hand stretch toward it.

The hatchling squealed and sprang away. It scrambled down the narrow internal stair to the dry stair section. Ekin jerked around and saw it flee below her. The hatchling kept going down the lower stair and reached the lower room’s dry ledge.

The mother whipped back defensively. Her body struck the nest, and the compass spun across the floor. It bounced onto the turning stair as a wave crashed against the lighthouse.

Water burst through the fixed sea-facing grate below. The lower room began to flood, and the rising wash caught the compass. Ekin watched the brass circle tumble through the lower room, flash once in the gray daylight, and wash through the iron grate into the sea.

“No!” Her cry vanished beneath the storm.

The mother charged after her hatchling, but she could not descend the narrow stair. Her shoulders met the walls at the turn. She pulled back and tried again, twisting her body, yet the tight bend stopped her. Ekin saw her remain trapped in the upper room, staring down toward the stranded hatchling.

Another surge foamed across the lower room. The little dragon pressed itself against the dry ledge above the floodwater.

Ekin descended the narrow internal stair to the dry section.

Duru followed down the same stair and reached the dry section beside her.

Neither girl entered the water. Ekin stared past the frightened hatchling at the grate where the compass had vanished. Her outstretched hand still trembled.

“I caused this,” she whispered.

Duru looked at the rising water below, then at Ekin. “We can’t get the compass back.”

Ekin swallowed hard. This time, she did not argue.

## SC05

Another surge struck the fixed grate, and water boiled below the dry ledge. The hatchling shrank against the wall, trembling.

Ekin dragged her gaze away from the flooding lower room. “I frightened it. I chased the compass, and now it’s trapped.” She faced Duru, forcing the words out while Duru watched her. “This is my fault, so I have to get it back to its mother.”

Thunder shook the lighthouse. The hatchling squeaked.

Ekin pulled off her jacket. “Duru, will you help me rescue it?”

Duru met her eyes and nodded. “Yes. Tell me what you need.”

Ekin crouched on the dry stair and held her jacket open toward the ledge. Duru watched as she lowered it within the hatchling’s reach.

“Come on,” Ekin murmured. “I won’t chase you.”

The little dragon sniffed the jacket. As water slapped near the ledge, it scrambled onto the cloth. Ekin folded the sides gently around its shivering body and drew it close.

The hatchling climbed from the lower ledge onto the lighthouse stair’s dry section.

Its claws shifted inside the jacket, and Ekin’s grip slipped. “Take it!”

Duru caught the bundled hatchling against her chest, steadying the handoff before it could tumble. Ekin saw her secure it and gave one sharp, grateful nod. “Good catch.”

Duru climbed the narrow internal stair and entered the upper room.

Held safely in Duru’s arms, the hatchling passed into the upper room.

Ekin climbed the narrow internal stair and entered the upper room behind them.

The mother dragon waited there, unable to squeeze down the tight turn. She bent toward the bundle as Duru lowered it. The hatchling wriggled free of Ekin’s jacket and pressed against its mother.

Ekin and Duru stood together in the dry upper room, breathing hard while the storm battered the lighthouse.

Ekin took back her damp jacket. “I couldn’t have done that alone.”

“I know,” Duru said, without teasing.

Ekin glanced at her. They were still strangers—but not quite as much as before.

## SC06

The hatchling pressed close to its mother. The sea dragon curved her body around the little creature, sheltering it from the wind that rushed through the open upper-room door. One broad wing settled over the hatchling until only its small face showed.

Ekin and Duru watched the pair nestle together in the dry upper room. The mother made no move toward them. She simply kept the hatchling safe beneath her wing.

Beyond the doorway, rain swept across the headland.

“My grandfather’s compass is still out there,” Ekin said. Her throat tightened, but she did not turn toward the sea. “I wanted to believe I could get it back.”

Duru waited, watching her.

Ekin drew a slow breath. “But it’s gone. Chasing it again won’t change that.” She looked at Duru. “You know the coastal route. I’ll follow your lead.”

Duru gave a serious nod. “Stay near me.”

Duru stepped through the open upper-room door onto the coastal path.

Ekin stepped through the open upper-room door onto the coastal path after her.

They set out together toward the harbor along the established coastal route. The storm continued throughout their return, driving rain against their faces and roaring over the coast. Both girls saw the dark clouds still churning above them, and both bent into the wind.

When Duru slowed, Ekin slowed. When Duru chose the safer footing, Ekin followed without arguing. In return, Ekin kept close enough that neither girl faced the storm alone.

At the end of the established coastal path, Duru entered the harbor.

Ekin entered the harbor behind her.

They stood there soaked and breathing hard. The brass compass remained lost at sea, and Ekin felt the emptiness of that loss. Yet when Duru glanced back to make sure she had arrived safely, Ekin met her eyes and nodded.

“Good route,” Ekin said.

“Good rescue,” Duru replied.

They were not best friends. Not yet. But they had crossed the storm together, and each now trusted the other to stay.
