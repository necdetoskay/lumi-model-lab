Selin crossed the school courtyard with her field notebook held firmly in both hands. The wind tugged at her jacket, but she bent her head and kept going. Dark rain clouds were gathering above the greenhouse.

A sudden gust struck from the side. The notebook snapped out of Selin’s hands before she could close her fingers around it.

“No!”

The wind carried the notebook across the courtyard and through the open greenhouse door. Selin saw it drop onto the greenhouse floor, its paper pages fluttering in the draft.

She hurried toward the greenhouse, choosing the most direct way.

“That way will take longer,” Bora called. He pointed toward the greenhouse side path. “Come around here. The side path goes straight to the entrance.”

“I can manage,” Selin said, although the first cold drops of rain were beginning to fall.

Bora started along the clear side path. “Then manage quickly.”

Selin glanced toward the longer way, then at the open greenhouse door. Her notebook still lay inside where she had seen it land. She pressed her lips together.

“All right,” she said reluctantly. “The side path is better. Lead on.”

Bora guided her around the side of the greenhouse, keeping to the clear path. Selin followed close behind, no longer wasting time trying to find her own route.

Together they reached the greenhouse entry. Selin stopped beside Bora and looked through the open door at her notebook on the floor, still out of reach.

Wind swept through the open greenhouse door, lifting the notebook’s pages and pushing it farther across the floor whenever Selin stepped toward it.

She started forward alone, then stopped. Chasing it might only drive it deeper inside. Rain tapped harder outside.

Selin looked at Bora. The words clearly cost her something. “Will you help me recover my notebook?”

Bora met her gaze and nodded. “Yes.”

He moved through the doorway first. When another gust rushed inside, Bora stepped between the open door and the notebook, using his body to shield it from the wind. The fluttering pages settled. Then he advanced slowly from one side, preventing the notebook from skittering away while leaving Selin a clear approach.

Selin saw what he was doing and followed. Bora held his position as she crouched and closed both hands around the same field notebook. He watched her lift it safely from the greenhouse floor.

For a moment, Selin hugged the notebook against her jacket. Then she faced Bora instead of hurrying away.

“That worked,” she said. “Your help worked. I wouldn’t have caught it so quickly alone.”

Bora gave a small, satisfied smile. “Good. We should get back before the rain gets worse.”

This time Selin did not argue. Keeping the notebook firmly in her hands, she walked beside Bora out of the greenhouse and along the clear side path. Together they returned to the school courtyard as the wind drove the approaching rain after them.
