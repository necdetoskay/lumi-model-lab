# Fenerdeki Hırsız — qualification candidate

## SC01

Ekin stood at the harbor wall, turning her grandfather’s brass compass between her hands. Beyond the boats, dark clouds gathered over the sea, but the water inside the harbor was still calm.

A sudden rush of wings whipped her hair across her face.

Something long and scaled swept over the wall. Before Ekin could close her fingers, the sea dragon snatched the compass from her hands. Brass flashed between its teeth.

“Hey!” Ekin scrambled onto the wall and raced along it after the dragon. “Give that back!”

The creature skimmed above the moored boats, then climbed toward the headland. Ekin jumped down onto the quay and kept running, dodging ropes and barrels without looking back.

“You won’t catch it that way,” someone called.

Ekin stopped only because the dragon was already pulling ahead. A girl she had never seen before stood nearby, pointing past the harbor. On the headland, the lighthouse rose against the darkening sky, its door open. The dragon was heading straight toward it.

“The lighthouse,” the stranger said. “That’s where it’s going.”

Ekin watched the dragon cross above the last boats, still carrying the compass. She hated that the girl was right.

“I know the coastal route,” the stranger added. “I’m Duru.”

“I didn’t ask for help.”

“No. But you need to reach the lighthouse.”

Ekin tightened her jacket and looked from Duru to the shrinking shape of the dragon. “Fine. We go together—but only until I get my compass back.”

Duru nodded. “Together, then.”

They turned toward the harbor road, ready to set out, while thunder muttered far across the water.

## SC02

Wind swept across the harbor, carrying the sharp promise of rain. Ekin stared toward the headland, where the lighthouse stood against the darkening sky.

“You’ll waste time if you go straight along the shore,” Duru said. “The coastal route bends above it. Follow me.”

Ekin frowned. Duru was a stranger, and strangers did not get to give her orders. But the sea dragon was already far ahead, carrying Grandfather’s compass.

“How do you know?” Ekin asked.

“I know the way to the lighthouse.” Duru pointed along the rising coast. “That route stays passable. We can reach the open door before the storm arrives.”

A cold gust tugged at Ekin’s jacket. She studied the direction Duru had shown, then gave a stiff nod. “Fine. The coastal route. But I’m only following because you know where you’re going.”

Duru accepted that without smiling. She started up the coast, and Ekin followed close enough not to lose sight of her, though not close enough to suggest trust.

The route climbed toward the headland as the wind strengthened. Duru chose each turn without hesitation. Ekin watched carefully, memorizing the way for herself. She would not depend on Duru longer than necessary.

They reached the lighthouse with the first drops of rain. Its door stood open. Duru went inside, and Ekin crossed the threshold after her.

They climbed through the lighthouse together until they entered the upper room. Ekin stopped beside Duru, breathing hard and listening to the storm gather outside.

“This doesn’t make us partners,” she said.

“No,” Duru replied. “Not yet.”

## SC03

Daylight spilled across the dry upper room as the sea dragon crouched inside it, filling nearly half the space. Ekin watched the brass compass gleam between its claws. Her grandfather’s compass was so close that she could make out every familiar scratch.

The dragon turned toward a nest against the wall. With a careful movement, it placed the compass in the nest.

Ekin stepped forward.

“Wait,” Duru whispered.

A tiny shape stirred beside the compass. Duru noticed it first and pointed. Ekin followed her gaze and saw a sea dragon hatchling in the nest, no bigger than a cat. It was uninjured, but its small wings could not lift it. It pressed close to the brass compass.

“That’s why the big one came here,” Duru said.

“I don’t care why it came.” Ekin kept her eyes on the compass. “I’m taking back what belongs to me.”

The mother dragon shifted at once. Still inside the upper room, it planted itself across the approach to the nest. Its broad body completely blocked Ekin’s path. Ekin stopped, watching its head track her every movement.

Duru glanced behind them. “We need to think.”

Both girls looked toward the narrow turning stair that led down to the lower maintenance room. From where they stood, they could also see the fixed iron grate facing the sea below.

Duru lowered her voice. “Don’t rush it.”

The compass flashed in the daylight, almost within reach. Ekin’s patience snapped tight.

“You keep warning me,” she muttered, “but that dragon has my grandfather’s compass.”

She leaned forward again, though the mother dragon still guarded the nest and held the upper room.

## SC04

The brass compass gleamed in the nest, close enough for Ekin to touch.

She stepped past the cat-sized hatchling and reached for it.

Duru saw her move. “Ekin, wait!”

Ekin’s fingers almost closed around the compass. The hatchling startled and scrambled between her feet. Its shoulder struck the nest, knocking the compass onto the floor. Then it fled toward the narrow stair.

“No!” Ekin cried.

She watched the hatchling hurry down the steps, unable to fly but quick on its small legs. Ekin followed, with Duru close behind. Above them, the mother sea dragon surged toward the stair. She tried to descend, but her body jammed at the tight turn. Her claws scraped against the fixed stone as she twisted and failed to force herself farther down.

Ekin saw her blocked there, filling the bend.

A wave crashed against the headland. Water burst through the iron sea grate into the lower room as the storm broke in full. The hatchling reached a dry ledge above the rising flood and crouched there, uninjured but trapped.

The compass bounced down the last steps.

Ekin lunged after it, but another rush of seawater swept across the lower floor. The brass compass spun away, flashed once beneath the grate, and washed through it into the sea.

“Grandfather’s compass!” Ekin froze, staring through the bars. It was gone.

Water surged higher around the base of the lower stair. Duru caught Ekin by the sleeve of her jacket and pulled her back before she could step into it.

“We have to stay above the water,” Duru said.

Ekin wanted to argue. Instead, she looked from the hatchling on the dry ledge to the mother trapped at the stair turn. Her reckless reach had sent the little dragon running—and now the compass was truly lost.

She backed up with Duru until both girls stood together on the dry section of the lighthouse stair. Rain and waves battered the headland, and below them the lower room continued to flood.

## SC05

Water surged through the lower room beneath the narrow stair, rising and falling with the storm. On a dry ledge beside the bottom step, the cat-sized hatchling crouched with its wings pressed tight against its body.

Ekin stared down at it. Above them, the mother dragon shifted restlessly in the upper room, too large to squeeze onto the stair.

“This is my fault,” Ekin said. The words hurt, but she made herself say the rest. “I chased them in here. I frightened the little one down the stairs. I have to get it back safely.”

Duru watched her for a moment, then nodded toward the ledge. “You can reach it from the last dry step. I’ll stay close.”

“I didn’t ask for instructions.”

“No,” Duru said. “But you might need help.”

Another rush of water swept across the lower room. The hatchling squeaked and flattened itself against the ledge.

Ekin pulled off her jacket. Keeping both feet on the dry stair, she crouched and stretched toward the hatchling. It shied away at first, but there was nowhere else for it to go.

“Easy,” Ekin murmured. She spread the jacket over its back and sides, then gathered the covered hatchling carefully against her chest. Duru saw the whole awkward rescue, including the moment Ekin nearly lost her balance.

Duru caught Ekin’s arm and steadied her. Then she slid her hands beneath the bundled hatchling while Ekin climbed back to a wider step.

“Don’t drop it,” Ekin warned.

“I won’t.”

Ekin looked at Duru’s firm grip, then met her eyes. “All right. I’ve got you.”

Together they adjusted the handoff until Ekin could support the hatchling again and Duru could guide its covered body away from the stair’s edge.

They climbed side by side, carrying it between them. At the upper room, the mother dragon lowered her head. The hatchling wriggled free of the jacket and hurried to her.

Ekin remained beside Duru. The storm still shook the lighthouse, but the space between the two girls no longer felt quite so sharp.

“Thanks,” Ekin said quietly.

Duru gave one small nod. “We did it together.”

## SC06

The mother sea dragon curved her long body around the hatchling, sheltering it from the rain blowing through the open upper-room door. The little dragon pressed close beneath her folded wing.

Ekin and Duru stood together and watched the pair settle safely in the dry room. The mother raised her head, keeping herself between the hatchling and the storm, but made no move toward the girls.

Ekin gazed beyond the doorway. The dark sea heaved under the rain. Somewhere in it, her grandfather’s brass compass was gone.

“I kept thinking I could get it back,” Ekin said.

Duru waited without interrupting.

“But chasing them nearly hurt the hatchling.” Ekin drew a shaky breath. “I can’t undo that, and I can’t recover the compass. It’s lost.”

Duru met her eyes. She had seen how hard the words were to say. “I’m sorry.”

Ekin nodded, accepting that instead of arguing. Wind drove another spray of rain through the doorway.

“We should take the coastal path,” Duru said. “It’ll lead us back to the harbor.”

This time, Ekin did not snap at her. “You know the route. Lead on—but don’t go too fast.”

Duru stepped through the open upper-room door, and Ekin followed her directly onto the established coastal path.

The storm continued throughout their return. Rain swept across the headland, and both girls saw the path shining ahead beneath the dark sky. When the wind pressed against them, Duru chose the steadier stretches. Ekin stayed close, calling out whenever the path became difficult to see.

Neither girl pretended the walk was easy. They moved carefully, checking on each other and keeping together until the harbor appeared through the rain.

At its edge, Ekin looked back toward the distant lighthouse. The compass was still lost, and the storm still rolled over the sea.

Then she turned to Duru. “I’m glad I didn’t make that walk alone.”

Duru gave a tired smile. Side by side, they entered the harbor.
