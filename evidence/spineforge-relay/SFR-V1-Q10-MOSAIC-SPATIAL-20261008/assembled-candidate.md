Rain rattled against the workshop roof, pushed by gusts that made the open gate look less inviting by the minute. The restored mosaic panel rested where it had been left, whole and ready for the gallery.

Aya stood beside it, studying its edges. “We’re losing time. I can carry the panel by myself.”

Mert watched her as she faced the panel and prepared to act. He remained between Aya and the wide workshop gate, keeping the approach blocked.

“No,” he said. “The safe way is through this gate into the covered yard. From there, the level covered ramp goes straight to the gallery entrance, and the open gallery door leads into the display alcove. We can keep the panel under cover for the whole route.”

Aya looked at him while he explained, then glanced past him at the gate. The rain beyond the covered way had thickened into slanting gray lines.

Mert did not move aside. “The gate boundary stays in place until we’re ready to carry it safely. You’re not taking the panel through alone.”

Aya’s mouth tightened. For a moment, she looked back at the mosaic as if impatience might make it lighter. It remained resting in the workshop, fragile and intact.

“All right,” she said, meeting Mert’s eyes. “I see the covered route: gate, covered yard, level ramp, then the open gallery door.”

Mert heard the reluctant acceptance and gave a brief nod, but he held his position at the gate.

Aya stayed beside the panel. She understood the route now, though the next words she needed had not yet been spoken.

The wind drove the rain harder against the workshop roof. Aya stood beside the restored mosaic panel, while Mert remained between her and the wide gate. The panel had not moved. Its repaired surface was intact, but its size no longer looked like a challenge she could win by being stubborn.

Aya studied it for another moment. Then she looked directly at Mert.

“Mert, will you help me carry the panel?” she asked. “We should carry it together, one of us on each side, and keep it steady along the covered route.”

Mert met her gaze. He had heard the request clearly. His serious expression eased, though he did not step away from the gate.

“Yes,” he said. “I’ll help you carry it. We’ll take our time and coordinate before we lift.”

Aya watched him give his answer and understood that he had accepted. A little of the tightness left her shoulders.

“Good,” she said. “I was trying to prove I could manage it alone. That would have put the mosaic at risk.”

A gust shook more rain across the covered yard beyond the wide gate. Neither of them approached the boundary. Mert still blocked Aya’s access to it, and the panel continued to rest in the workshop.

Aya looked from Mert to the fragile mosaic. Asking had cost her a few seconds, but those seconds no longer felt wasted.

“We do this carefully,” she said.

Mert nodded. “Together.”

They stayed where they were, ready to plan their shared hold before making any attempt to lift the panel.

Rain rattled harder above the workshop, and wind swept through the wide gate. Mert still stood between Aya and the approach. The restored mosaic panel remained intact where they had left it.

“Take your side,” Mert said. “We lift on three, then stop and check our hold.”

Aya almost answered that she was ready without checking. Instead, she placed herself opposite him and waited.

“One,” Mert said.

Aya watched his stance and matched it.

“Two.”

They each set both hands carefully against their own side of the panel.

“Three.”

Aya and Mert lifted the mosaic panel together. Its full weight came into their shared hold, steady between them. Neither tried to take more than the other.

“Hold there,” Mert said.

Aya resisted the urge to move ahead. She adjusted only enough to keep her side level, following his pace. The panel stayed intact.

“Mine is steady,” she said.

“So is mine.”

For a moment they remained still, carrying the panel together while rain drummed beyond the workshop. Aya looked toward the gate, but she did not rush for it.

Mert shifted his body away from the gate approach. The movement visibly opened the safety boundary that he had been maintaining.

Aya saw him release the yard gate approach, and she accepted that the way was now clear. She kept her feet planted inside the workshop and maintained her side of the shared hold.

“Good,” she said. “I’ll wait for your count before we move.”

Mert gave a brief nod. “No sudden turns.”

Aya looked across the intact mosaic at him. “No rushing.”

They remained in the workshop with the panel securely carried between them, the gate boundary released and the covered route waiting beyond.

Rain hammered above the workshop and swept past the wide gate in slanting sheets. Aya held her side of the restored mosaic level. Across from her, Mert kept his side steady.

“Ready?” he asked.

Aya felt the old urge to answer by stepping out first and making him catch up. The fragile panel between them stopped her. “Ready. You set the pace.”

Mert began a slow count. “One step. Then another.”

Aya moved through the wide workshop gate into the covered yard. She stopped before her arms could pull ahead.

Mert moved through the wide workshop gate into the covered yard. He kept his shoulders square to hers.

The mosaic panel moved through the wide workshop gate into the covered yard between them. Their shared hold remained stable, and the restored surface stayed intact.

Wind drove rain beyond the covered route, loud enough to make the yard seem smaller. Aya’s first instinct was to hurry away from the noise. She tightened her attention instead of her grip.

Mert took one careful step.

Aya matched that step exactly.

He took another, no faster than before. Aya matched his pace again, keeping her side level rather than trying to lead the shared carry.

Mert watched her feet, then looked across the panel at her. He had seen that she was no longer surging ahead.

“That’s steady,” he said.

Aya gave a short nod. Praise from him still felt strange, but not unwelcome. “Then steady is how we keep going.”

They paused together in the covered yard, still holding the same intact panel between them. Rain beat harder outside their shelter, but neither of them rushed toward the ramp.

Rain drummed harder over the covered yard, and wind sent a cold spray across the edge of the sheltered route. Aya and Mert still held the restored mosaic between them, each supporting one side.

The level covered ramp stretched toward the open gallery door. Aya looked along it and felt the urge to hurry. Then Mert adjusted his next step, and the panel shifted by the smallest amount between their hands.

“Can we go slower?” Aya asked. “I want us to keep the panel level.”

Mert looked directly at her. He had heard the request, and his serious nod showed that he understood.

“Slower,” he agreed.

He shortened his next step and waited before taking another. His pace settled into a careful rhythm for their shared carry.

Aya watched him make the change. Only when she was certain she could follow that rhythm did she move.

Aya crossed the level covered ramp from the covered yard to the gallery entry. She kept her side from rising or pulling ahead.

Mert crossed the level covered ramp from the covered yard to the gallery entry. He matched the slower shared pace all the way.

The same fragile mosaic panel crossed the level covered ramp from the covered yard to the gallery entry between them. It remained level, restored, and intact in their two-person hold.

At the entry, they stopped together. The wide gallery door stood open ahead, but they did not pass through it.

Aya breathed out carefully. “That was better.”

Mert glanced at the panel, then back at her. “Better than rushing.”

Rain rattled behind them. Aya did not argue.

Rain beat harder beyond the gallery entry, and wind drove spray against the open doorway. Aya and Mert held the same restored mosaic between them, still level and intact.

The fixed display mount waited in the alcove. Aya studied the remaining distance. Her arms ached with the temptation to finish quickly, but she remembered the tiny shift on the ramp.

“Mert, help me keep it level all the way to the mount,” she said. “Same slow pace?”

He met her eyes and nodded. “Same pace.”

Aya stepped through the wide open gallery door into the display alcove, keeping both hands steady on her side.

Mert stepped through the wide open gallery door into the display alcove, matching her careful movement.

The mosaic panel passed through the wide open gallery door into the display alcove, supported between them in their two-person hold.

They stopped before the mount. Neither tried to take control.

“Down together,” Aya said.

“Together,” Mert answered.

Aya and Mert lowered the mosaic panel onto the fixed display mount at the same careful speed. They eased their hands away only after it rested securely. The restored panel remained intact, now displayed where it belonged.

For a moment, Aya listened to the rain hammering outside. Then she turned to Mert.

“I wanted to carry it alone at first,” she said. “That would have been a mistake. Your help—and both of us being careful—got it here safely.”

Mert watched her as she spoke. His shoulders loosened, though he only gave a small nod.

“We did all right,” he said.

Aya looked back at the intact mosaic. “We did.”
