Ekin rubbed her thumb across the dent in her grandfather’s brass compass. The harbor wind smelled of salt and rain, and beyond the masts, clouds piled dark over the sea.

Something green flashed beneath the water.

A long neck shot over the quay. Ekin glimpsed silver eyes, wet scales, and teeth closing around the compass—not her fingers, but close enough that she felt the snap.

“Hey!”

The sea dragon plunged away. Its ridged back sliced between the fishing boats, heading for open water.

Ekin raced along the quay, dodging coils of rope. “Give it back!”

She reached the last mooring post as the dragon vanished behind the breakwater. Ekin planted one foot on the post, ready to leap onto a departing boat.

“Unless you can swim faster than that, don’t.”

A girl Ekin had never seen before crouched beside a rain barrel. She pointed to the water. Three torn strands of dark-green weed clung to the stones, mixed with chips of pale shell.

“So?” Ekin snapped.

“That weed only grows around the headland.” The girl stood. “And those shells come from the caves below the lighthouse.”

Ekin stared toward the distant headland. The lighthouse stood white against the bruised sky, its open doorway a tiny black mark.

“I know the coastal way,” the stranger added. “I’m Duru.”

“I didn’t ask for a guide.”

“No. You were busy trying to jump into the harbor.”

Thunder muttered offshore. Ekin clenched her empty hand. “Fine. We go to the lighthouse together. Until I get my compass back.”

Duru nodded once. They turned toward the harbor road as the first cold drop of rain struck the stones.
