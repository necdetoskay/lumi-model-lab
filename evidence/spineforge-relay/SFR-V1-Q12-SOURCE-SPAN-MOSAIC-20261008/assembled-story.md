# Q12 — Mosaic delivery

## S01

Rain drummed harder above the workshop, and wind pressed a restless hiss against the wide gate. The restored mosaic panel rested where it had been left, its bright pieces joined into one fragile whole.

Aya stood near it, studying its size. Waiting made her shoulders tighten.

“I can carry the panel by myself,” she said. “If I move now, I can get it there before the rain gets worse.”

Mert watched her as she leaned toward the panel. He stayed between Aya and the approach to the wide gate, keeping the safety boundary in place. He did not step aside.

“No,” he said. “The route is covered, but that doesn’t make a solo carry safe. We have to go through this wide gate into the covered yard. Then the level covered ramp leads to the gallery entry, and the gallery door is already open beyond that.”

Aya straightened and looked at him while he explained. Mert remained in her path to the gate.

“The turns and passages are wide enough if the panel stays level,” he continued. “But you can’t cross the gate with it while I’m holding this boundary, and I’m not releasing it for one person to carry the panel alone.”

Another gust drove the strengthening rain against the roof. Aya glanced from the resting mosaic to the gate, impatience still plain in her face. Then she pictured the covered yard, the level ramp, and the open gallery door in the order Mert had described.

“All right,” she said. “I see the covered route. Gate, covered yard, level ramp, then the open gallery door.”

Mert heard her acknowledgment and gave a small, serious nod. He still blocked the approach. Aya stayed in the workshop beside the intact panel, no longer arguing that the route itself was the problem, but not yet asking him to help.

## S02

The rain beat faster overhead, and a gust rattled the wide workshop gate. The restored mosaic panel remained where it rested, intact and waiting.

Aya looked at the panel, then at Mert. He still stood between her and the approach to the gate. The safety boundary remained in place.

Her first impulse was to argue again. She wanted to prove that she could manage the weight and reach the gallery without losing time. But the panel’s joined pieces were fragile, and wanting to be quick did not make a solo carry careful.

Aya let out a slow breath.

“Mert, will you help me carry the panel?” she asked. “I mean together, with both of us holding it through the covered route.”

Mert heard her request. His guarded expression eased, though he did not move away from the gate.

“Yes,” he said. “I’ll help you carry it. We’ll share the hold and keep it level.”

Aya watched him as he accepted. The answer took some of the tightness from her shoulders.

“Good,” she said. “We should agree on every move before we make it.”

“We should,” Mert replied. “And neither of us rushes the other.”

Aya gave a short nod. They were still classmates, and the strain between them had not vanished. But she had asked instead of insisting, and he had answered without turning it into a victory.

Outside, wind drove the strengthening rain across the covered yard. Inside, neither of them lifted the mosaic. Mert continued blocking the approach to the wide gate, keeping the safety boundary active while the same intact panel rested in the workshop.

## S03

Rain drummed harder beyond the workshop, and the wind pressed against the wide gate. Mert remained between Aya and the approach, keeping the safety boundary in place.

Aya faced him across the same restored mosaic panel. “Ready?”

“On three,” Mert said. “Lift evenly, then hold.”

Aya resisted the urge to start early. She set herself at one side while Mert took the other.

“One,” he counted. “Two. Three.”

Aya and Mert lifted the mosaic panel together. It rose level between them, intact, with both of them maintaining a steady two-person hold.

The weight pulled at Aya’s arms. She almost shifted ahead, but Mert said, “Stay there. Let me clear the approach first.”

Aya stopped herself and followed his coordination. “All right. I’m holding.”

Still supporting his side of the panel, Mert changed his bodily position. He stepped away from his blocking place and opened the approach to the yard gate, explicitly releasing the safety boundary.

Aya saw him move clear. The way to the gate was no longer blocked, but she did not rush toward it.

“Boundary released,” Mert said. “We move only when we’re both set.”

Aya checked that their shared hold remained level. Rain swept across the covered yard outside, louder now, but the panel stayed safely inside the workshop.

“We’re both set,” she said, keeping her side steady. “And we go together.”

They remained in the workshop with the same fragile panel raised between them, ready for the next coordinated move.

## S04

Rain rattled across the covered yard as Aya and Mert faced the wide workshop gate. The same restored mosaic panel stayed raised between them, each supporting one side.

“Together,” Mert said.

Aya tightened her hold without pulling ahead. “Together.”

They carried the panel through the wide workshop gate into the covered yard in one coordinated crossing, keeping their shared hold steady and the fragile mosaic level between them.

A gust drove rain past the edge of the covering. The sound made Aya want to hurry toward the ramp, but Mert’s steps remained measured. For one pace, she moved faster than he did, and the panel’s edge began to angle.

Aya caught herself before the tilt grew. She slowed to Mert’s pace, matching each careful step while maintaining her side of their shared carry. The panel settled level again, still intact.

Mert watched the change in her movement. His gaze went from her feet to the even line of the panel, and some of the tension left his face.

“That pace works,” he said.

Aya nodded. Her arms ached, and rain hammered harder nearby, but she did not try to take the lead. “Keep it steady. I’ll stay with you.”

They remained together in the covered yard, both hands still supporting the mosaic. The level covered ramp waited ahead, but neither of them started onto it. For now, they held their position and kept the restored panel balanced safely between them.

## S05

Rain drummed harder beyond the covered yard, and the wind pushed damp air across the level ramp. Aya and Mert kept their shared hold on the restored mosaic panel. It remained level and intact between them.

Aya looked along the covered route to the open gallery door. Her arms were beginning to ache, but rushing now would only make the fragile panel harder to control.

“Mert, can we carry it more slowly on the ramp?” she asked. “I need us to keep the same pace.”

Mert heard her request and met her eyes. He gave a short nod. “Yes. You set the first step, and I’ll match you.”

Aya moved one foot forward without advancing onto the ramp. Mert adjusted his stance to the same measured rhythm, clearly slowing his movements to match the pace she had requested. Aya watched him settle into it. The pull on her side eased, and the mosaic stayed even.

“Like that,” she said.

“Like that,” Mert agreed.

Still supporting their opposite sides, Aya and Mert carried the same fragile panel together along the level covered ramp from the covered yard to the gallery entry, maintaining their shared hold for the entire crossing.

Rain beat against the covered route, louder now, but neither of them hurried. At the gallery entry, they stopped together before the wide open door. The panel remained raised between them, restored and intact.

Aya released a careful breath. “That was better.”

Mert glanced at the even edge of the mosaic. “Slower was better.”

They stayed at the entry with both of them still carrying it. Beyond the open door, the fixed display mount waited in the alcove, but they did not take the panel inside yet.

## S06

Rain hammered the covered route behind them, and wind drove the sound through the gallery entry. Aya kept both hands steady on her side of the mosaic panel. Across from her, Mert maintained the same careful hold.

“The door is wide enough,” Aya said. “Same pace as before?”

Mert nodded. “Same pace.”

Together, without loosening their shared hold, Aya and Mert carried the restored fragile panel through the wide open gallery door from the gallery entry into the display alcove. Their steps stayed slow and matched. The panel remained level and intact between them for the whole crossing.

They stopped in front of the fixed display mount.

“Ready to lower it?” Mert asked.

Aya checked his stance, then met his eyes. “Ready. Together.”

Aya and Mert lowered the mosaic panel onto the fixed mount. They guided it into place at the same time, keeping their hands on it until it rested securely. Only then did Aya release her side. Mert released his. The same restored panel stood intact on display.

For a moment, they both looked at the finished mosaic while the rain beat harder outside.

Aya flexed her tired fingers. “I wanted to do this alone at first,” she said. “That would have been a mistake.”

Mert turned toward her and listened.

“You matched my pace, and you helped keep it steady all the way,” Aya continued. “We got it here safely because we carried it carefully together. Thank you.”

Mert gave a small, tired smile. “You kept it steady too.”

Aya nodded. They were still classmates, not suddenly best friends, but the trust between them no longer felt like a guess.
