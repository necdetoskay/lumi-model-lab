Aren knew his garden very well. He knew the crooked path, the soft green moss, and the leaves that tickled his elbows. But today something was different. The garden was quiet. It was too quiet.

Aren looked up. A small gray cloud hung low above him, very still.

His tummy gave a little wiggle. He could run inside. But he wanted to know why his garden felt so hushed. So Aren stayed. He closed his eyes and listened to the garden he knew. Then he began to hum the soft garden sounds: hmm, hmm, swish, swish, hmm.

The cloud trembled. Then one tiny, gentle drop fell near his feet. Plip.

"Hello," said Aren softly. "What is your name?"

The cloud hung there, thinking. "I do not know," it whispered.

Aren could have said, "I think it is Puff!" But that would be making things up, and he did not want to do that. "That's all right," he said kindly. "I will just call you Cloud."

Cloud sighed, happy and relieved, and floated down a little closer. "Thank you, Aren. I have forgotten something else too. I have forgotten my song."

"Oh," said Aren. He thought hard. "Maybe we can look for sounds that help you remember."

Past the end of the crooked path was the Commons. Aren had never been there. His feet felt tiny and his heart felt big. He took a deep breath and stepped forward, and Cloud drifted along right beside him.

The Commons was full of soft sounds. Aren heard a tick-tick. He heard a swishy whisper. He heard a low, sleepy hum. He did not know who or what was making them, so he only said what he heard. "I hear a tick-tick, Cloud. And a swish. And a hum."

Cloud listened too. Its edges began to glow, just a little, like the sky at morning.

"Do you want to try singing?" asked Aren.

Cloud made a first note. It was wobbly. It went up, and then down, and then squeaked. Aren did not say, "No, not like that." Instead he hummed along with the wobble, up and down and squeaky too.

Cloud giggled. Aren giggled. Their giggles bumped together, and out of Cloud came a small, soft drizzle, pitter, pat, pitter, pat, like a tiny drum.

"I remember!" said Cloud. "The beginning of my song!"

"Let's take it home," said Aren. "The garden would like to hear it."

They walked back along the crooked path to the garden he knew. The moss was waiting. The leaves were waiting. Everything was still quiet.

"You can sing it your own way," said Aren. "I will wait."

Cloud was quiet for a moment. Maybe it was looking for its name, too. But the name did not come, and that was all right. Aren did not guess. He just waited, and Cloud felt safe.

Then Cloud sang. It sang the beginning, and the middle, and the very end. Gentle rain fell on the moss and the leaves and the crooked path. Plip, plop, pitter, pat. The garden woke up in a happy hush.

Aren tipped his face up and smiled. His garden did not feel quiet anymore. It felt like a song.

"Thank you, Aren," said Cloud.

"You're welcome, Cloud," said Aren. And Cloud's name was still a secret, waiting to be found, until the day Cloud found it.
