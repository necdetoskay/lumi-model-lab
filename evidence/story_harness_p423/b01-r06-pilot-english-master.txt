Little Mira sat by the window in her cozy home. In her hand lay a small stone. It was a shining stone, but tonight it was dim and quiet, like a sleepy eye.

"Why don't you shine?" Mira whispered. She looked out into the dark night. "Maybe a light is lost out there."

Mira felt a little flutter of fear in her tummy. Hiding under her blanket would be easy. But Mira was curious, and Mira was kind. She closed her fingers around the stone, gently and tightly. She opened the door very slowly. Creeeak. Then she stepped outside, and her house stayed behind her, small and warm.

Soon her feet touched soft grass. She had come to a meadow she had never seen before. The grass swayed, and the night was big and still.

The stone was still dim. Mira did not sit and wait for it to wake up by itself. The stone would not do that. So she cupped it between her two hands to warm it, just like warming a little bird. Then she hummed a soft lullaby. "Hmm, hmm, hush-a-bye, little stone, shine bright."

Slowly, slowly, the stone began to glow. Its light fell on the blades of grass and made a tiny path in front of Mira. "Thank you," said Mira, and she followed it.

At the end of the path, something twinkled low in the grass. It was a little star. Its light was shaky, and it looked very sad.

Mira could have tucked the star into her pocket to keep it for herself. But that did not feel right. She knelt down and asked softly, "Little star, where do you belong?"

The star answered in a trembly voice. "I want to go home. I remember a window with a warm, golden light."

Mira did not know who the stone belonged to, and she did not know who owned anything out here. She did not pretend to. She only knew that she could help. "Then let's look for that window together," she said.

Mira held the stone's light out in front of the star, so the star would not feel scared. She walked in tiny, slow steps, and the star floated close by her side. Behind them, the dark part of the meadow grew small. Ahead, the way home began to shine. Together they turned toward the house.

Mira's legs felt tired, and her eyes felt heavy. But she kept going, one small step, then another. At last they reached the window of her home, where the warm light spilled out onto the grass.

Mira gently set the star down beside the window. The star sat in the golden glow and let out a happy sigh. Its light grew round and bright and steady. "This feels like home," it said.

Mira smiled. She still did not know who the stone belonged to. That was a secret for another day. So she kept it close, safe and warm in her hands.

Then Mira went inside her cozy house, and the little star shone happily in the window all night long.
