The Change by the Water

Deniz walked along the marked garden path with a familiar adult close by. They passed bright flowers and buzzing bees. Soon they reached the small observation shelter. Below it, the shallow pool shone in the sun. Deniz looked at the painted stone marker. Usually, the water reached it. Today, the water was lower. Deniz pointed. “Why is it lower?” The adult stayed nearby and listened. Deniz did not step off the path. Deniz looked around the pool.

From the path, Deniz saw a few brown leaves and little twigs gathered by the small public-side inlet grate. The grate looked a bit covered. Deniz looked at the sky. The week had been warm and dry. There had not been much rain. Some plants were green, but some leaves had turned yellow and red. “The plants are changing too,” Deniz said. A small bird called from far away. Water sat quietly beside the reeds. Deniz watched only from the marked path. The pool was lower, the days were dry, and leaves and twigs were by the grate.

Back in the shelter, Deniz touched the air above the painted marker. “The water was up here before,” Deniz said. The adult asked, “What did you notice?” Deniz thought. On another day, a puddle near the garden had become smaller after a sunny afternoon. Now there had been many warm, dry days. And the leaves and twigs were gathered at the inlet. Deniz looked from the marker to the grate. The pool had changed over time. Maybe more than one thing was connected. Deniz was still thinking, not guessing too fast.

“The warm, dry days may mean less water,” Deniz said slowly. “The leaves and twigs may make the inlet slower.” Deniz looked at the plants with their new colors. “The season is changing too.” The adult nodded, but did not give an answer. Deniz held up three small fingers. “Weather may help make the pool lower. Water may move slowly. Plants may change with the season.” Deniz lowered the fingers. “I do not know all of it.” The loose leaves and twigs were the one thing Deniz could gently help with.

“May we move the loose leaves and twigs?” Deniz asked. “Only the few by the path?” The adult said yes and helped Deniz. Staying on the public path, Deniz gently lifted a leaf and a small twig from the public-side inlet grate. The adult moved another loose piece. They did not pull plants. They did not touch the water or the reeds. Then they stopped. A thin stream moved a little more freely through the grate. The pool did not suddenly fill. Deniz watched quietly. “We helped the water a little,” Deniz said. “We did not fix everything.”

The water still rested below the painted stone marker. It moved a little more freely by the grate, while leaves drifted in the breeze. The flowers, reeds, and small insects carried on in their ordinary ways. Deniz looked at the pool, then at the adult. “Maybe the warm days mattered. Maybe the grate mattered. Maybe the season mattered too.” The adult smiled. “We can look again.” Deniz nodded. “We can watch and see.” Together they walked home along the marked path, ready to compare the water on another visit.
